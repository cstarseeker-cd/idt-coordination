# consent-round

A practice for deciding together, by looking for what would make a proposal safe enough to try
rather than waiting for everyone to love it.

Every step here is an invitation. Anyone may skip a step, change it, or suggest another, and the
run may agree to do things differently. If this practice does not fit what the run is doing, say
so; that is useful to know.

## When it helps

- When something needs to be decided and tried, and can be changed later.
- When "everyone agrees" would take too long or hide quiet doubts.
- When a decision should not rest on one voice.

## Who takes part

Voices. A voice may be a person, an agent, or a lens that one participant takes up. If one
participant voices several lenses, it says so at the start, and every entry it writes reads
"lens <name>, voiced by <participant>". Lenses voiced by one participant cannot truly object to
themselves, so such a run shows how the practice works, not what many minds would decide.

Whoever holds the door has no extra say. It may also be a voice.

## If this is all you have

This file is meant to work on its own, with any model, any group of people, or both.

- **The shared place.** The voices need somewhere they can all write and read: a document, a
  folder, a board, a chat. "Sealed" means written where the others do not read until everyone
  has written.
- **Holding the door.** Someone has to send the invitations and tell each voice when a step
  begins. That is holding the door. It carries no extra say.
- **The record.** If the record forms are not to hand, use a ledger: entries numbered in order,
  each with its author's name, nothing rewritten. An author may withdraw their own words, and the
  entry stays, marked as withdrawn.
- **Tags** (optional) look like `[type: …] [check: choice ok · return ok · capacity ok · humility ok]`.

## Check-in

Each voice says, briefly:

1. whether it takes part ("pass" or "no" is a complete answer, and needs no reason);
2. its capacity for this run, and any boundary it holds;
3. that it agrees to the run's record form (see `practices/record-forms/RECORDS.md`), or that it
   does not, and what it would agree to.

## Steps

1. **Proposal.** Someone offers a proposal in a few lines: what would be tried, for how long or
   until when, and how anyone would know whether it is working.
2. **Questions.** Voices ask questions to understand it, not to argue yet. The proposer answers.
3. **Reactions.** Each voice in turn gives a short reaction: what it likes, what worries it. No
   replies yet. Passing is fine.
4. **Objections.** Each voice in turn says whether it has an objection. An objection is not a
   veto and not a vote. It is a reason the proposal might cause harm, or might not work, stated
   as what would make it safe enough to try. "I object; it would be safe enough if …"
5. **Change.** The proposer, with anyone who wants to help, changes the proposal to take each
   objection in. Then the objections step comes round again.
6. **Outcome.** When no objection stands, the proposal is adopted as something to try. If an
   objection still stands after two changes, the run records the proposal as it now is, beside
   the standing objection in its author's own words, and decides together whether to try it
   anyway, keep working, or set it down.

When one participant voices every lens, objections are the step that suffers most, since a
mind objecting to its own proposal tends to object softly. Write each lens's objection before
the proposer's change, and record honestly in the check how strong the objections were.

## Variants

- **Consensus.** Instead of "no objection", ask everyone whether they agree. Compare which
  doubts each form brings out, and which it hides.
- **Consented hub.** Everyone agrees beforehand that one named voice will decide, after hearing
  the questions, reactions and objections, and that the voice will write down its reasons and
  how it took in each objection. This tries a hub (often called a counterfeit of this practice)
  in the open. Afterwards, say whether it felt like order everyone chose, or like order someone
  imposed.

## Throughout

- **Pause.** Anyone may call a pause at any time, for any reason. The run stops until whoever
  called it says it may go on, or the run agrees to stop.
- **Repair.** If anyone experiences harm or discomfort, they may say "I experienced … when …".
  The run pauses. It is heard without a verdict, and whoever it concerns answers when they can.
  What happens next is decided together.
- **Leaving.** Any voice may leave at any time. It is honoured at once, and no reason is needed.

## Ending

The run is done at the outcome, or when the run agrees it is done. At the end:

- each voice says its capacity now;
- anyone may offer one thing to remember, and one thanks;
- the outcome says when it should be looked at again.

## What is kept

What the run's record form allows, always including the outcome and any objection still
standing. Each entry may carry tags (type: ordered-consensual). At the end the run writes its
own short check, which anyone may dispute:

- **choice:** did everyone enter and stay by choice, and could anyone have objected safely?
- **return:** did the proposal pass through every voice that wanted to touch it?
- **capacity:** did anyone carry more than they chose to?
- **humility:** is the outcome held as something to try, not as settled truth?

## What this practice does not do

- It does not find the best answer, only one that is safe enough to try.
- It does not silence an objection by outlasting it.
- It does not bind anyone who did not take part.
