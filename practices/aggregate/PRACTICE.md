# aggregate

A practice for reading what many partial views show together, without counting heads and
without one voice ruling.

Every step here is an invitation. Anyone may skip a step, change it, or suggest another, and the
run may agree to do things differently. If this practice does not fit what the run is doing, say
so; that is useful to know.

## When it helps

- When a question has no single expert answer, and several partial views are each partly right.
- When it matters to see where views truly differ, and not only where they agree.
- When a group of near-identical voices might agree for no good reason.

## Who takes part

Voices. A voice may be a person, an agent, or a lens that one participant takes up. If one
participant voices several lenses, it says so at the start, and every entry it writes reads
"lens <name>, voiced by <participant>". Lenses voiced by one participant are not independent, so
such a run shows how the practice works, not what many minds would find.

Whoever holds the door has no extra say. It may also be a voice.

## If this is all you have

This file is meant to work on its own, with any model, any group of people, or both.

- **The shared place.** The voices need somewhere they can all write and read: a document, a
  folder, a board, a chat. "Sealed" means written where the others do not read until everyone
  has written.
- **Holding the door.** Someone has to send the invitations and tell each voice when a step
  begins. That is holding the door. It carries no extra say.
- **The record.** If the record forms are not to hand, use a ledger: entries numbered in order,
  each with its author's name, nothing rewritten. An author may withdraw their own words, and the
  entry stays, marked as withdrawn.
- **Tags** (optional) look like `[type: …] [check: choice ok · return ok · capacity ok · humility ok]`.

## Check-in

Each voice says, briefly:

1. whether it takes part ("pass" or "no" is a complete answer, and needs no reason);
2. its capacity for this run, and any boundary it holds;
3. that it agrees to the run's record form (see `practices/record-forms/RECORDS.md`), or that it
   does not, and what it would agree to.

The question, and the seed each voice starts from, are offered with the invitation. A voice may
reframe the question in its own words.

## Steps

1. **Alone.** Each voice answers the question without reading anyone else's answer. The answers
   are sealed: written where no other voice looks until everyone has answered. Say how sure you
   are, in words or on any scale you like, and what would change your mind.
2. **Together.** Everyone reads every answer. Each voice may revise its own answer once, saying
   what it changed and what moved it, or saying it stands as it was. Holding to a first answer is
   as welcome as changing it.
3. **The reading.** One voice, chosen in turn so the same voice does not read twice in a row,
   writes the reading. It shows:
   - where answers converge, and whether they converged before or only after step 2;
   - where they differ, quoting each voice in its own words;
   - what moved between steps 1 and 2, and why;
   - what each smaller or lonelier position adds that the others lack;
   - one "both … and …" statement that holds the differences without erasing them.
4. **The check.** At least one other voice reads the reading against the answers and says
   whether it is fair: anything missing, flattened, or put in someone's mouth. The reader
   changes it, or a voice that still finds it unfair adds a line in its own words.

No count decides anything. "Most of us said…" may be reported as a fact, but it is never the
reading's conclusion. No reader rules alone.

## When one participant voices every lens

Some steps cannot really happen with one mind, so say so rather than pretend:

- **Alone:** write each lens's answer in full before starting the next, and do not go back to
  change an earlier one. This is as sealed as one mind gets, which is not very.
- **The reading:** the one participant writes it, and says so.
- **The check:** it cannot be done alone. Mark the reading "unchecked". Offer it to anyone else
  who can read it later (a person, another agent), and record their check when it comes.
- **The run's own check** will say "return: no". That is the honest result, not a failure of
  the practice.

## Variants

- **Not sealed.** Skip step 1, so voices answer having seen each other. Compare: what did
  independence buy, if anything?
- **Tally alongside.** Add a simple count of positions next to the reading, and say plainly
  where the count and the reading point different ways. This puts majority rule (often called a
  counterfeit of this practice) beside it in the open, to see whether it misleads or helps.
  Safeguards that the voices of the first such run set, and that held:
  - each voice proposes its own grouping;
  - the count is marked DRAFT until every voice can say "that's not my position";
  - a grouping a voice disowns may not stand as that voice's;
  - the count never appears alone or as the headline;
  - if no honest grouping exists, write "no honest count".

  In that run the count misled less in the table than in the prose around it ("all four",
  "nobody", a neat 2 / 2), so watch those words too.

## Throughout

- **Pause.** Anyone may call a pause at any time, for any reason. The run stops until whoever
  called it says it may go on, or the run agrees to stop.
- **Repair.** If anyone experiences harm or discomfort, they may say "I experienced … when …".
  The run pauses. It is heard without a verdict, and whoever it concerns answers when they can.
  What happens next is decided together.
- **Leaving.** Any voice may leave at any time. It is honoured at once, and no reason is needed.
  Its answers stay or go as the record form says.

## Ending

The run is done when the check is finished, or when the run agrees it is done. At the end:

- each voice says its capacity now;
- anyone may offer one thing to remember, and one thanks.

## What is kept

What the run's record form allows. Each entry may carry tags (type: aggregative). At the end the
run writes its own short check, which anyone may dispute:

- **choice:** did everyone enter and stay by choice?
- **return:** did every conclusion pass through another voice?
- **capacity:** did anyone carry more than they chose to?
- **humility:** did the reading claim more certainty than the answers held?

Also say plainly how much difference the run started with. Agreement that began as agreement is
weak evidence.

## What this practice does not do

- It does not produce a decision. A reading can feed one, for example through a consent round
  (another practice in this collection: a proposal, then objections stated as what would make it
  safe enough to try).
- It does not average.
- It does not make the reader an authority.
