## The reading

### Where they differ, and what dropped

The differences are about where each voice puts the test of a reading.

- logs, step 2: weigh by independence of source and keep contradictions open: "treat counts and authority as just two more signals."
- misused, step 2: independence too, and a sharper aim: "say what the signals rule out rather than settle what they don't."
- retelling, step 2: the test is that the source recognises its part: "Return to source, not count or rank." And recognition "checks the telling, not the world."
- changed, step 2: the test is correction by those described: "Correction by those it concerns amends the entry without erasing the earlier one."

On agreement. These are four different ways of handling it, and I do not sort them into bins.
- logs and misused give agreement a weight set by how independent the sources were.
- retelling traces agreement and does not count it. Its own words: "'These two reached the same place by different roads' is worth saying. 'These three said the same thing in the same words' is a warning." It says that is weighing by independence in words, not numbers, and it says on this axis it sits nearer logs and misused.
- changed: "I do not weigh agreement at all" (changed/aggregate-2.md, as I quoted before).

Source or subject. misused/aggregate-2.md separates retelling and changed: "The person doing the recognising is the signal's source... The person correcting is the subject, who may not be a source at all." I think that is a real distinction and I keep it separate below. retelling said it would split from changed if changed objects. changed has not said either way in the files I have.

What dropped between step 1 and step 2, per changed's check (I did not re-read):
- changed's own POSITION: step 1 said "truth is what survives their correction, not what most sources say" and "provisional". Step 2 drops both. (My earlier line that changed added "attribution is itself provisional" is still true of its detail, and I leave the two side by side without reconciling them.)
- misused: the step-1 POSITION said "treat disagreement as information, not noise". Step 2 has "keep disagreement open" in its place. Whether that is a drop or a rewording is misused's to say. The step-1 habit 4, "A lonely position counts for most ...", is not repeated in step 2.
- logs: I believe my POSITION did not change. I have not re-checked mine against the others' reading of it.
- retelling: changed's check lists no drop for it. I have not looked.

Because of these drops, my earlier remark that everything that moved was an addition is withdrawn. That sentence made the field look more settled than changed's account does. The drops are the part worth reading for whether the voices were leaning toward each other. I still cannot tell openness from leaning from the files.

Open and unresolved: removal. misused/aggregate-1.md worried that no-rewrite "is itself a place where misuse can happen". In step 2 misused kept the rule but wrote "I'm still not sure a visible withdrawal mark removes the stigma." My logs/aggregate-2.md left removal "as an open question." It stays open.

Open and unresolved: absent voices and the no-rewrite rule. This is changed's item 11, which it calls "the most consequential open point". As I read it: retelling offers the written trace as the witness for voices that are absent. The ledger's no-rewrite rule means that trace is never deleted, and a correction goes in beside it. But the people changed lists as unable to correct ("the people described cannot reach the record, or are not safe to correct it") are the ones who cannot add that correction. So the trace speaks for them and stays, and they cannot amend it. I have not resolved this and I don't know whether it can be resolved inside the ledger. I said in reading.md I "only touched" absent voices. It belongs here, not under "left out".

Partial remedies exist, so "none of us has a remedy" is withdrawn. retelling offers the written trace for absent voices, and in step 2 an outside anchor for checkable claims (changed's item 6). Both are partial. The first runs into the tension above.
