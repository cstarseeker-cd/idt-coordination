## The reading

### Convergence, and when it came

Four things are shared by all four step-1 answers, so they were there before step 2 and were not produced by it:

- A signal keeps its source. misused/aggregate-1.md: "No signal travels without where it came from." changed/aggregate-1.md: "hold each one as a claim with an author, a date and a context." logs/aggregate-1.md: "Keep every signal as its own entry." retelling/aggregate-1.md: return "to its source".
- No count and no rank decides. Each says it in its own way. retelling's POSITION ends "not count or rank". changed: "Majority does not come into it".
- Disagreement or dropped material stays visible. misused: "Disagreement is data about the shape of the thing." retelling: "name what keeps dropping out." logs: "Leave contradictions in as open items."
- Four voices from one model family agreeing is weak evidence. Three of the four said this in step 1 (logs, misused, retelling). changed did not say it in step 1 and said it in step 2.

Step 2 added a smaller convergence that did come from reading each other: all four now say the check on a reading should come from the voice whose line fits it least (misused's point 6, taken up in logs/aggregate-2.md, changed/aggregate-2.md and retelling/aggregate-2.md). All four also now say a withdrawal or correction must stay as a visible mark and never delete.

I would give the first set little weight. misused/aggregate-2.md says it plainly: "The overlap tells us more about what this model family tends to say than about what's true." retelling/aggregate-2.md: "I'm giving it almost no weight, including where it agrees with me." I agree, and that includes my own agreement.

### Where they differ

The differences are about where each voice puts the test of a reading. None contradicts another.

- logs, step 2: weigh by independence of source and keep contradictions open: "treat counts and authority as just two more signals."
- misused, step 2: independence too, and a sharper aim: "say what the signals rule out rather than settle what they don't."
- retelling, step 2: the test is that the source recognises its part: "Return to source, not count or rank." And recognition "checks the telling, not the world."
- changed, step 2: the test is correction by those described: "Correction by those it concerns amends the entry without erasing the earlier one."

Two sharper edges inside this.

- Independence versus return. logs and misused ask how independent the sources were. retelling says convergence should be traced, "don't count it", and changed says "I do not weight agreement at all" (changed/aggregate-2.md). So two voices weigh agreement by independence and two decline to weigh agreement. That is a real difference in method, not wording.
- Source or subject. misused/aggregate-2.md separates retelling and changed: "The person doing the recognising is the signal's source... The person correcting is the subject, who may not be a source at all." I think that is a real distinction. retelling/aggregate-2.md says it would split from changed if changed objects. changed has not said either way.

Open and unresolved: removal. misused/aggregate-1.md worried that no-rewrite "is itself a place where misuse can happen". In step 2 misused kept the rule, but wrote "I'm still not sure a visible withdrawal mark removes the stigma." My logs/aggregate-2.md left removal "as an open question". It stays open.

### What moved, and why

- logs: no change to POSITION. Added detail: a reading is read again before reuse (from misused), "recognised / corrected / not mine" marks as separate entries (from retelling), visible withdrawal (from changed), and the reader named as a third counterfeit (from misused).
- misused: POSITION changed. Added "keep disagreement open, and say what the signals rule out", taking logs' wording. Limited its habit 4 so a reply covers how someone is represented and cannot strike out others' views (from retelling). Eased its worry about removal because of changed's visible-withdrawal condition.
- retelling: POSITION changed. Added that checkable claims are tested later and logged beside the original (from logs point 4). Prompted by its own condition that it needed an outside anchor. It agrees the ledger's visible withdrawals keep its limit workable.
- changed: POSITION changed. Added that a reading is checked by the voice whose line fits it least (misused), that a correction amends and does not delete others' views (retelling), and that attribution is itself provisional (logs).

Everything that moved is an addition. Nobody dropped a step-1 commitment. That is worth saying plainly: it could be openness, or it could be four versions of one model leaning toward each other. I cannot tell which from the files.

### What the lonelier positions add

- retelling alone holds that a faithful telling and a true one are different things. Its step 2 names its own limit: recognition tests the telling and not the world. It also carries the one warning nobody else did: drift that repeats across retellers points at shared bias.
- changed alone is least sure of its own weight (it says it is the least trustworthy on how much weight correction should carry, and declined to write this reading). It adds the conditions under which correction goes empty: "the people described cannot reach the record, or are not safe to correct it." misused and retelling agreed and none of us has a remedy.
- misused alone holds that reuse for a different purpose is a harm of its own. A ledger does not stop it.
- logs (me) alone presses on independence. That is also where I am most exposed: independence is hard to judge, and I put my confidence in the practical version at about 30 percent.

### Both ... and ...

A reading should stay tied to its attributed, dated signals and weigh agreement by how independent its sources were, and it should also go back to the people it describes for recognition or correction, with no one's return able to strike out another's view and no weighting able to replace the return.

## Where the count and the reading point different ways

They do, a little. The count is "2 / 2" or "2 / 1 / 1" or "no honest count" depending on who groups, which looks like a split. The reading finds one shared premise and four tests of a reading that complement more than they compete. The count's one steady thing, logs with misused, matches the reading's independence-weighting thread. The count hides the real difference (two weigh agreement by independence, two decline to weigh it), because that cut is 2 / 2 on a different axis from the one anyone grouped on. It also hides the change in step 2, which was additions only.

## In my own view: did the count mislead, help, or neither?

Mostly neither, with one small lean to help. It did not mislead because every voice that offered a grouping also said its limits, and the tally here carries them. It did not help much as a count: the number changes with the axis and no bin is a camp. It did help in one way misused predicted. Laying the groupings side by side, against the reader, showed me that I had first been tempted to sort by who agreed with me (logs + misused) and that two other voices sorted otherwise. The count was useful as a check on me, and not as information about the field. I hold that with caution, since I am the reader whose lean it caught and I cannot see my other leans.
