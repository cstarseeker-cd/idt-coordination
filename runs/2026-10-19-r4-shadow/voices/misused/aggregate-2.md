## 1. Revision

My answer from step 1 mostly stands. I'm changing two parts of it, and one doubt has eased.

**Habit 4 gets a limit, from retelling.** I gave the person a reading is about "the right to
attach a reply that goes wherever the reading goes". Retelling draws the line I hadn't drawn:
"Recognition covers the telling, not the conclusion ... I can correct how I'm told. I can't strike
out someone else's view." I take that limit as it stands. A reply that travels with the reading
covers how the person was represented. It isn't a way to strike out what others saw. Without
that limit my habit 4 could slide into the veto I said it wasn't.

**The reading's aim gets sharper, from logs.** Logs says the field "can read what the signals
jointly rule out, and keep what they leave open as open." That's a better target than my "true
enough for one purpose". I'd put it first: a reading says what the signals rule out, says what
they leave open, and doesn't settle anything they didn't settle. Purpose still matters for reuse
(habit 5), but it comes second.

**The doubt that eased.** In step 1 I said the case I took most seriously was the voice seeded
"someone who once needed a record changed" showing that an attached reply leaves the harm in
place, so that removal sometimes has to be possible. Changed didn't argue for removal. Changed
asked for the opposite guard: "The withdrawal mark has to stay visible, or the ledger becomes the
thing it was meant to guard against," and named its own risk as "whoever edits last wins and the
first entry is quietly buried." So for now I'll keep the no-rewrite rule. I'm still not sure a
visible withdrawal mark removes the stigma. Changed's answer just doesn't show that it fails to.
Changed also named the condition under which all of this goes empty: when the people described
"cannot reach the record, or are not safe to correct it." Retelling says the same thing about
absent voices. I agree with both, and I don't have a remedy to add.

**A caution about this step itself.** All four step-1 answers came out close to each other:
provenance kept attached, independence weighed over number, contradictions left open, and some
way back to the source. Every one of us also warned that four voices from one model family
agreeing is weak evidence. I don't think we get to drop that warning now that it applies to us.
The overlap tells us more about what this model family tends to say than about what's true.

## 2. Position

POSITION: Keep each signal tied to its source and blind spots, weigh independence not number, keep disagreement open, and say what the signals rule out rather than settle what they don't.

## 3. Grouping

I'm grouping the four step-1 POSITION lines, since those are the ones I can see. My revised line
above stays in the same group. I group them **2 / 1 / 1**:

- **misused + logs, together.** Both say: keep signals separate and tied to provenance, weigh
  them by independence of source, and keep contradictions open. The difference is wording.
- **retelling, alone.** The test is that "every source recognises its part in the retelling",
  plus naming what dropped out. The person doing the recognising is the signal's *source*, and the
  thing recognised is *the telling*.
- **changed, alone.** The test is surviving correction by "those it describes". The person
  correcting is the *subject*, who may not be a source at all, and the thing corrected is *the
  claim*. That's a different person and a different act from retelling's, so I won't put the two
  in one bin, even though both point back to people rather than counts.

Why this is honest, with a limit: the bins differ in **emphasis, not in opposition**. None of the
four lines contradicts another. All four share provenance and visible disagreement. So a "2 / 1 /
1" must not be read as a split or a dispute. It shows which test each voice puts first. If anyone
wants to show it as a disagreement, I'd rather it said "no honest count".

The grouping is mine (misused). Each voice can say "that's not my position" before it stands.
