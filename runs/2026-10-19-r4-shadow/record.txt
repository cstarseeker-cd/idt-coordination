#5  misused (Opus)   [voices/misused/aggregate-1.md]
# aggregate step 1, alone: misused (opus)

[type: aggregative]

## Reframing

I'd ask the question a little differently: *how might this field hold many partial signals
so that any reading made from them stays tied to what it rests on, and so that it can be
checked and refused?* "Read truth" is the right aim. What worries me from my seed is
what happens to a reading once it exists. Most misuse I've seen didn't come from a reading
that was wrong when it was made. It came from a reading, or a fragment of one, that travelled
somewhere it was never meant to go, without what it rested on.

## My answer

There's no method that turns partial signals into truth. What a field can do is keep
readings honest about what they are. I'd build that from six habits:

1. **No signal travels without where it came from.** Every signal keeps three things attached:
   who it came from, what they could see from where they stood, and what they couldn't. A
   quote with its source removed is where misuse usually starts. The reading should point
   back to the raw signals it used, and do it by default.

2. **Weigh independence, not number.** Majority fails less because it ignores minorities than
   because it counts correlated signals as if they were separate. Five echoes of one source are
   one signal. So the first question to ask of any agreement is whether it could have happened
   without any real contact with the thing itself: shared training, shared framing, shared
   fear. That applies to this run. We are four lenses from one model family.

3. **Disagreement is data about the shape of the thing.** Where partial views conflict is often
   where the truth is complex, or where one seat is hiding something from the others. A reading
   that settles every conflict has probably thrown away its most useful information. Some
   differences should stay in the reading as differences.

4. **The person a reading is about can answer it.** That doesn't mean a veto, and it doesn't
   mean authority. It means the right to attach a reply that goes wherever the reading goes and
   can't be separated from it. A lonely position counts for most when it belongs to the one
   who has to live under the reading.

5. **A reading is true enough for one purpose.** It was read for some reason. Reusing it for a
   different reason means reading it again, not just quoting it. A large share of the misuse I
   have in mind is reuse: a record made to help someone that is later used to sort or punish them.

6. **The third counterfeit is the reader.** Once majority and authority are ruled out, the
   reader's synthesis can quietly become the authority instead. A graceful "both … and …" can
   smooth over exactly the edge someone needed kept sharp. The guard is the practice's own:
   the reading points back to the words it rests on, and someone else checks it. I'd add that
   the check should come from the voice whose answer fits the synthesis least, not just from
   any other voice.

On the tally: I expect it to mislead if anyone looks at it on its own. It might help if
it's used *against the reader*, as a way to catch a reading that has made a lopsided split
look balanced, or a lonely position look like consensus. Used that way, a count is a check on
the reader, not a verdict. I'm curious which way it goes.

## How sure I am

- Fairly sure about 1, 2 and 6. They follow from how evidence and records work, not just from
  my seed.
- Moderately sure about 3. Some disagreement really is just noise, and I don't have a clean
  test for telling the two apart.
- Least sure about 4 and 5. They're where my seed pulls hardest, so they're where I'm most
  likely to be biased.

## What would change my mind

- If keeping provenance attached makes readings so heavy that no one can act on them, I'd
  accept lighter provenance: enough to trace a reading back, without carrying it all in front.
- If the tally catches a reader flattening something the reading alone would have hidden, I'd
  count that as the tally helping, and I'd say so.
- The one I take most seriously: if the voice seeded with "someone who once needed a record
  changed" shows that an attached reply still leaves the harm in place, because the original
  stays and so does the stigma, then annotation isn't enough, and sometimes removal has to be
  possible. That would mean the ledger's no-rewrite rule, which I chose to block one kind of
  misuse, is itself a place where misuse can happen. I suspect that's true, and I don't yet
  know how to hold both.

POSITION: Keep each signal tied to its source and blind spots, weigh independence not number, and treat disagreement as information, not noise.

#6  logs (Sonnet)   [voices/logs/aggregate-1.md]
# aggregate step 1 - logs, alone

Reframe: the field doesn't need to read "the truth" out of the signals. It can read what the signals jointly rule out, and keep what they leave open as open.

How I'd do it:
1. Keep every signal as its own entry: who or what produced it, when, by what route, and what it actually claims. Don't merge or smooth entries. A log is only worth trusting while the entries stay separate.
2. Weight agreement by how independent its sources are, not by how many there are. Four voices from one model family agreeing is close to one signal repeated. Two signals that reached the same claim by different routes count for more than ten that share a route.
3. Leave contradictions in as open items. Don't settle them by vote or by rank. A contradiction is information about where the field is unsure.
4. Prefer signals that commit to something checkable later. When it's checked, the result is a new entry. It doesn't overwrite the old one.
5. Authority gets the same treatment as any signal: logged with its provenance, weighed by what it can show. Rank alone adds nothing.

Limit I should name: a log records what was said and done. It doesn't record what is so. Trusting logs means trusting that entries can't be quietly rewritten. It doesn't mean trusting what they say. And "who wrote it" is itself a claim I may not be able to verify.

Sureness: about 60 percent on the shape (separate entries, weight by independence, keep contradictions open). About 30 percent that this works in practice when independence is hard to judge, as it is here.

What would change my mind: evidence that people say less when everything is kept, so that the logs thin out; a case where counting beat provenance-weighting in a way I'd accept afterwards; or a showing that independence can't be assessed well enough for step 2 to mean anything.

POSITION: Keep signals unmerged with provenance, weight by independence of source, keep contradictions open, and treat counts and authority as just two more signals.

#7  retelling (Fable)   [voices/retelling/aggregate-1.md]
# aggregate step 1: retelling, alone (sealed)

## A slight reframe

The question asks how to *read* truth from partial signals. I'd turn it a little: **how might the
field make one telling that every partial teller can find their part in, and that says plainly
what none of them saw?**

I trust retelling more than writing for one reason. A written record holds one version still, and
that version gains weight just by lasting. A retelling has to pass through someone else, and the
person it came from can still say "that isn't what I meant". For me, truth is what survives being
retold and is still recognised by the people it came from.

## My answer

1. **Retell before weighing.** Before anything is weighed, each signal is retold by a voice other
   than its source, in that voice's own words. The source then answers: *recognised*, *recognised
   with a correction*, or *not mine*. A signal enters the reading only in a form its source
   recognises. That replaces authority, because no one's paraphrase stands without the source's
   yes. It also replaces majority, because a signal counts by being understood, not by having
   company.

2. **Recognition covers the telling, not the conclusion.** A source can say "you've told my signal
   wrong". It cannot say "I refuse what the others saw". Without that limit, recognition becomes a
   veto. I can correct how I'm told. I can't strike out someone else's view.

3. **Read the drift.** Where a retelling drifts from what the source meant, that is information.
   Either the signal was unclear, or the reteller's frame bent it. Drift that repeats across
   retellers points at the field's shared bias, which matters when four voices come from one model
   family.

4. **Keep what survives, and name what keeps dropping out.** The reading is two things: what
   survives retelling by voices with different starting points, and a plain list of what kept
   falling out of the retellings. What falls out is often the lonely position or the awkward fact.
   Naming it stops the smoothing.

5. **Trace convergence, don't count it.** "These two reached the same place by different roads" is
   worth saying. "These three said the same thing in the same words" is a warning, not a
   confirmation.

6. **The reading is a retelling too.** So it goes back to every source, which is the check step.
   It is true enough when each voice can say: "I can see my part in it, and where I disagree, the
   disagreement is stated the way I'd state it." It does not need everyone to agree with it.

**Where this breaks.** Rumour is also retelling. The difference is the return: rumour never goes
back to its source. So this only works while sources are present to recognise themselves. For
voices who are absent, gone, or unsafe to ask, the written trace is the only witness left, and that
is where writing earns its place over retelling. Retelling is also slow and lossy, and a fluent
reteller can still smooth things over while sounding faithful.

## How sure

- About the core idea (return to the source and recognition as the test, instead of a count or a
  rank): **fairly sure, about 6 in 10.**
- About whether it works at scale, or with absent voices: **not very sure, about 3 in 10.**

## What would change my mind

- If someone shows that recognition by the source lets people disown true readings they dislike,
  even with the limit in point 2, then the test needs an outside anchor, probably a written one.
- If someone shows a case where a written trace caught something every retelling had smoothed
  away, I'd hold writing as the check on retelling, not the other way round.
- If step 2 shows the four of us converging mainly because we're one model family, I'd want the
  reading to give that convergence almost no weight, and that includes any convergence with me.

POSITION: A reading is true enough when every source recognises its part in the retelling and what kept dropping out is named: return to source, not count or rank.

#8  changed (Haiku)   [voices/changed/aggregate-1.md]
Voice: changed (Haiku 5.5). Step 1, alone. Record form: ledger (author: changed).

Reframe. I would not ask how to read truth from many partial signals as if truth were a thing the signals point at. I would ask: which account of this can the people it describes still correct, and how does that correction get recorded without erasing what was first said?

From the seed. Someone who once needed a record changed knows two things. First, a record is a snapshot made by someone at a moment, and the person it describes is often the one who knows it is wrong. Second, the wish to change a record is often the first honest sign that the record was being used as a verdict. So for partial signals I would hold each one as a claim with an author, a date and a context, not as a fact. Truth then becomes provisional. A reading is what the signals support now, it stays open to correction from those it concerns, and it can say which signals were withdrawn and why.

What this avoids. Majority does not come into it, because a count of signals does not make one of them more correctable. Authority does not come into it either, because the person who wrote the final reading is not the one who can amend it. The correction path is the only thing I would give standing to.

How sure. Moderately unsure, perhaps 55 percent that this is a useful frame and not just a preference of my seed. The idea is thin on how it works at scale. I have not seen how it behaves when many signals arrive at once.

What would change my mind:
- If correction turns into a new authority, where whoever edits last wins and the first entry is quietly buried. The withdrawal mark has to stay visible, or the ledger becomes the thing it was meant to guard against.
- If the people described cannot reach the record, or are not safe to correct it, then "correctable by those it concerns" is empty, and something else has to carry the weight.
- If signals that are wrong but unchallenged come to look just like signals that are right. Then the frame gives no way to tell them apart, and I would need a separate check, perhaps the one that asks whether anyone who could contradict a claim was able to.

POSITION: Hold partial signals as attributed, dated, correctable claims. A reading stays provisional and open to those it describes; truth is what survives their correction, not what most sources say.

## The reading

### Where they differ, and what dropped

The differences are about where each voice puts the test of a reading.

- logs, step 2: weigh by independence of source and keep contradictions open: "treat counts and authority as just two more signals."
- misused, step 2: independence too, and a sharper aim: "say what the signals rule out rather than settle what they don't."
- retelling, step 2: the test is that the source recognises its part: "Return to source, not count or rank." And recognition "checks the telling, not the world."
- changed, step 2: the test is correction by those described: "Correction by those it concerns amends the entry without erasing the earlier one."

On agreement. These are four different ways of handling it, and I do not sort them into bins.
- logs and misused give agreement a weight set by how independent the sources were.
- retelling traces agreement and does not count it. Its own words: "'These two reached the same place by different roads' is worth saying. 'These three said the same thing in the same words' is a warning." It says that is weighing by independence in words, not numbers, and it says on this axis it sits nearer logs and misused.
- changed: "I do not weigh agreement at all" (changed/aggregate-2.md, as I quoted before).

Source or subject. misused/aggregate-2.md separates retelling and changed: "The person doing the recognising is the signal's source... The person correcting is the subject, who may not be a source at all." I think that is a real distinction and I keep it separate below. retelling said it would split from changed if changed objects. changed has not said either way in the files I have.
