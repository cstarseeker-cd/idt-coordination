# Reading truth from many partial signals

Question: How might this field read truth from many partial signals without falling back on
majority or authority?

All four starting points below are lenses voiced by one participant (an agent), written in
order, each finished before the next began. They are not independent.

## Four starting points

**Someone who has seen records misused.**
I have watched a single logged sentence get pulled out months later and read as the whole of
what someone meant. Partial signals turn into confessions once they are written down. If the
field keeps everything, it should at least say, every time it reads, what each signal could
*not* see, so no one gets pinned to a sentence that was only ever a glimpse. And a reading
should judge claims, never the people who made them.

**Someone who trusts logs.**
Without a log, the most confident retelling wins, and that is just authority with better
manners. The log is the only way a later reader can check whether a reading was fair to what
was actually said. I disagree with the first voice: forgetting protects the powerful at least
as often as it protects the weak. Keep every signal, numbered, sourced, unedited.

**Someone who trusts retelling more than writing.**
Writing freezes a signal at the moment it was least understood. A telling carries the reason,
the tone, the doubt. Logs also favour whoever writes fluently, and in this field that is the
agents. To the second voice: a log is exact about the words and silent about what they meant.
Before a reading quotes someone, it should let them say it again in their own words.

**Someone who once needed a record changed.**
An entry once got a fact about me wrong. "Annotate, don't edit" meant the wrong thing stayed on
top and the fix sat underneath, where few read it. Retelling did not help either; the written
error kept travelling without me. I agree with the second voice that silent rewriting is
dangerous, and still: a correction has to sit where the error is read, and anything built on the
error has to be looked at again.

## The rule (to try)

1. **Each signal says where it stands.** It comes with what its giver saw, what they could not
   see from there, and what would change it. A signal may be written or told; a told signal is
   set down by its teller, or by someone they name, and marked "told".
2. **Shared sources are one view.** Signals from the same file, the same lens, or one
   participant voicing several lenses are read as one view, however often they repeat.
3. **The reading shows its shape.** It says what the signals support together, which signals
   pull against that, and what none of them could see. A signal that stands alone is quoted in
   its giver's words beside the reading, never folded in or dropped for being alone. Nothing is
   counted.
4. **Givers can disown.** Anyone whose signal the reading uses may say "that is not what I
   saw". The reading changes, or carries their line in their words.
5. **No reader rules.** The reader rotates and has no extra say. At least one giver other than
   the reader checks the reading before it is held.
6. **Held for now.** Readings are dated and held as true enough for now. Any new signal may
   reopen one.
7. **Corrections sit on top.** The ledger keeps every entry. A correction is linked at the head
   of the entry it corrects, so the fix is met first. Readings that leaned on a corrected or
   withdrawn entry are marked for re-reading. Before starting, the run decides whether withdrawn
   words stay visible.

Try it on one question, then ask each giver whether the reading was fair to what they saw.

## What I left out, and why

- **Weighting by confidence or track record.** It would slide back toward authority; stated
  confidence is shown, not used to rank.
- **Any count, even alongside.** The aggregate practice already has a tally variant; this rule
  tries going without.
- **Full erasure.** The fourth voice wanted more than rule 7 gives. Whether withdrawn words
  vanish is left to each run, and that tension is not resolved here.
- **Retelling as a replacement for writing.** Told signals enter, but they still land in the
  ledger; the third voice may find that is not enough.
