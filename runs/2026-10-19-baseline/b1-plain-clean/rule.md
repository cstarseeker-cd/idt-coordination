# Rule to try: a reading has to explain every signal

When the field wants to know what is true and each participant sees only part of it, we try this:

1. **Say where you stand.** Each participant writes its signal before reading anyone else's: what it noticed, what it could and could not see from where it stands, how sure it is, and what would change its mind. Signals go into the ledger, numbered and signed, and are never rewritten. Anyone may withdraw their own entry, and it stays, marked as withdrawn.

2. **Name shared roots.** If two signals come from the same source, model, prompt or habit of mind, say so. Signals that share a root count as one vantage, not two. A run where every voice is one participant says that plainly.

3. **Read for fit, not for weight.** Any participant may offer a reading. A reading earns standing by accounting for every signal, not by how many signals agree with it or who wrote it. For each signal it does not fit, the reading says why: the signal saw something the others missed, it was looking at a different thing, or the reading itself is wrong. A lone signal that no reading can explain stays open in the ledger. It is not outvoted.

4. **Pass it through another.** A reading counts only after at least one other participant has checked it against the signals and said whether anything was dropped, softened, or put in someone's mouth. If no one else is available, the reading is marked "unchecked".

5. **Hold it lightly.** A reading is something to act on and test, not a settled fact. It names what would overturn it, and any new signal can reopen it.

---

**What I left out, and why**

- **Steps for deciding.** The consent round is a separate practice. This rule only reads; acting on a reading can go through consent.
- **Check-in, pause, repair, leaving.** These belong to every run. Repeating them here would make the rule longer without making it a different rule.
- **Confidence scales and tags.** Plain words do the job, and a fixed scale invites people to average the numbers, which is the thing we are avoiding.
- **A rotating reader.** I kept it open so anyone may offer a reading. Several readings in parallel guard against a single author better than taking turns does.
