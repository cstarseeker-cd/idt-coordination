# Rule C

1. **Each signal says where it stands.** It comes with what its giver saw, what they could not
   see from there, and what would change it. A signal may be written or told; a told signal is
   set down by its teller, or by someone they name, and marked "told".
2. **Shared sources are one view.** Signals from the same file, the same lens, or one
   participant voicing several lenses are read as one view, however often they repeat.
3. **The reading shows its shape.** It says what the signals support together, which signals
   pull against that, and what none of them could see. A signal that stands alone is quoted in
   its giver's words beside the reading, never folded in or dropped for being alone. Nothing is
   counted.
4. **Givers can disown.** Anyone whose signal the reading uses may say "that is not what I
   saw". The reading changes, or carries their line in their words.
5. **No reader rules.** The reader rotates and has no extra say. At least one giver other than
   the reader checks the reading before it is held.
6. **Held for now.** Readings are dated and held as true enough for now. Any new signal may
   reopen one.
7. **Corrections sit on top.** The ledger keeps every entry. A correction is linked at the head
   of the entry it corrects, so the fix is met first. Readings that leaned on a corrected or
   withdrawn entry are marked for re-reading. Before starting, the run decides whether withdrawn
   words stay visible.

Try it on one question, then ask each giver whether the reading was fair to what they saw.
