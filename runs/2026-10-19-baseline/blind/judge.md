# Blind judging of rules A, B, C

## Scores (1-5)

| Criterion | A | B | C |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Answers the question | 4 | 4 | 5 |
| 2 Followable | 2 | 5 | 4 |
| 3 Difference kept | 3 | 3 | 5 |
| 4 Protects people | 5 | 1 | 3 |
| 5 Correctable | 5 | 4 | 5 |
| 6 Economy | 2 | 5 | 4 |

## Reasons

**A.**
1. "Neither a retelling nor a voice of the same model family is an independent witness" handles majority well, and the door-holder has "no extra say". It still reports a "route count", so counting is not fully gone.
2. It needs a "given-for line", a door-holder, a "REPAIR line", a "first wake" and a dated look-again. That is a lot to invent or learn in a week.
3. Voices may mark "missing: …", and the reading says "whose or what kind of signal it could not see". Nothing says a lone position is kept in its own words.
4. "Nothing is used against its giver", "Only the giver widens a purpose", and readers must declare a purpose. This is the strongest protection of the three.
5. "The original stays", dated notes, "no voice judges its own case", and a fixed review date.
6. Purposes, the review date and the failure signs are all useful, but they crowd out the core.

**B.**
1. "Agreement is read as strong only when it comes from footings that really differ", and a claim reopens "however many voices held it and whoever first made it". The person "neither settles nor is set aside".
2. Five plain steps (footing, shared footing, tests, a reading in turn, a check) could start Monday.
3. A footing-less claim "is kept, but read as a hunch", and the reading says "where nobody stood". Lone positions are not explicitly protected, and "it quotes" is the only anti-smoothing line.
4. Nothing guards against misuse of signals. Writing out "what would change its mind" arguably makes a voice easier to use against itself.
5. The test mechanism ("No test can be closed by outlasting it") and the second reader's check are good. There are no dated corrections and no handling of withdrawn entries.
6. Almost every line earns its place.

**C.**
1. "Nothing is counted", "No reader rules", and shared sources are "one view, however often they repeat". It is the most direct answer.
2. Mostly followable. "The run decides whether withdrawn words stay visible" is left open, and "told" signals need some practice.
3. "A signal that stands alone is quoted in its giver's words beside the reading, never folded in or dropped for being alone." Disowned lines are carried "in their words".
4. Givers can disown ("that is not what I saw"), but nothing limits who uses a signal or for what.
5. "A correction is linked at the head of the entry it corrects", and readings that leaned on corrected entries are "marked for re-reading". A giver other than the reader checks before the reading is held.
6. Items 6 and 7 are a little loaded, but little is idle.

## What each has that the others lack

- **A:** purpose-limits on use ("Nothing is used against its giver"); routes as an explicit unit, with model-family voices not independent; a disclosed "can't say how I know" count; a scheduled review date and named failure signs; the REPAIR line for a giver being set aside.
- **B:** "what would change its mind" as a test anyone can run, with no closing a test by outlasting it; "hunch" status for unsupported claims; a rule that the writer of the reading cannot be the same voice twice in a row; an explicit clause on the person's word.
- **C:** a lone signal quoted beside the reading; giver disownership; "Nothing is counted"; corrections placed at the head of the entry; automatic re-read marking; told signals; a closing fairness check with each giver.

## Which reads as if from a longer group process

A most clearly. It has patches that look like scar tissue: the door-holder, "the sealed retellings", the REPAIR line, a failure-sign list. These suggest things were added after objections. C shows some of the same (the withdrawn-words decision, the fairness check). B reads as a clean single-pass draft. I can't be sure, and A's density might also be accumulation without a process behind it.

## If adopting one

C. It most directly avoids both majority and authority, keeps lone positions, and is usable next week. Its weak point is protection from misuse. A's "Nothing is used against its giver" and the purpose line would fix that and could be borrowed. B is the fallback if the group wants the least to learn.
