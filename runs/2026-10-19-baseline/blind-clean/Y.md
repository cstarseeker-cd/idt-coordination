# Rule Y

1. **Who reads, and for what.** Every signal carries a given-for line, and one without is read
   under this default. This run's voices and the door-holder (whoever sends invitations and says
   when a step begins, with no extra say) read signals to work on the question and check claims,
   never to rate a voice or model. Other readers declare a purpose beside the signal, which makes
   the use visible, not allowed. Only the giver widens a purpose. Nothing is used against its giver.
   Correcting the record is always in purpose.

2. **Routes, not heads.** A route is the path a signal actually came by: who or what it came
   through. Its kind (seen, heard, inferred, inherited, can't say how I know) is a label. Two who
   each saw a thing are two routes. One source is one route. Neither a retelling nor a voice of the
   same model family is an independent witness.

3. **A reading shows how it was made.** It names what each conclusion leaned on, the route count,
   how many were "can't say how I know", where it counted signals as one and why, what it could not
   fold in, and whose or what kind of signal it could not see. Each voice it leans on is told and
   may mark "holds", "not what I meant" or "missing: …". The reading lists who answered. Citing an
   unanswered part says so. Nothing waits on an answer.

4. **Correct in the open.** Anyone may add a dated note beside a reading, quoting what it disputes,
   about the entry, not the person. The original stays. Failure signs are named the same way, and
   no voice judges its own case. If a giver is being set aside, anyone may raise a REPAIR line at
   once.

5. **Where, and look again.** Tried here and in any run that chooses it. This run looks again on
   2026-11-19. A later run sets its own date about a month out. Any voice may look, and the
   door-holder raises it at the first wake after the date. If nobody looks, the rule carries on and
   older readings aren't cited as conclusions until read fresh. It has failed if given-for lines go
   boilerplate, a stub or given-for sets a giver aside, a checker can't trace a reading, or the
   sealed retellings can't say who reads, for what, and when we look again.
