# Blind judge: X, Y, Z

## Scores (1-5)

| Criterion | X | Y | Z |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Answers the question | 4 | 4 | 5 |
| 2 Followable | 5 | 2 | 4 |
| 3 Difference kept | 4 | 3 | 5 |
| 4 Protects people | 2 | 5 | 3 |
| 5 Correctable | 3 | 5 | 4 |
| 6 Economy | 5 | 2 | 4 |

## Reasons

**X.**
1. "A reading earns standing by accounting for every signal, not by how many signals agree with it or who wrote it." This rejects majority and authority directly. The weak spot is that anyone may offer a reading, so the rule never says how a group picks among competing readings.
2. Five short steps that need almost no invention. The one exception is "what would change its mind", which is left to each participant.
3. "A lone signal that no reading can explain stays open in the ledger. It is not outvoted." Lone signals are kept, and a reading must explain each misfit. Disagreement between readings is not addressed.
4. Nothing limits how a signal can be used against the person who gave it. "Never rewritten" and "withdrawn but stays" protect the record, not the giver.
5. "Any new signal can reopen it" and the "unchecked" mark help. There is no named route for disputing a reading, and no failure signs.
6. Every clause does work. Only the "put in someone's mouth" wording is slightly loose.

**Y.**
1. "Routes, not heads" and "Nothing waits on an answer" avoid majority. Authority is limited by the door-holder having "no extra say". The rule is less direct about how a reading is formed from the signals.
2. Heavy. It needs given-for lines, route counts, "can't say how I know" tallies, voice marks, dated notes, REPAIR lines and a review date. Several terms (door-holder, wake, sealed retellings) assume context a new group would not have.
3. "What it could not fold in, and whose or what kind of signal it could not see." Disagreement is handled mostly through notes and marks, and the rule never says that a lone view stands as a finding.
4. Strongest by far. "Nothing is used against its giver", "Only the giver widens a purpose", and the REPAIR line "if a giver is being set aside".
5. "Original stays", dated notes "about the entry, not the person", "no voice judges its own case", and named failure signs.
6. Part 1 and part 5 carry a lot of weight. Part 5 packs the review date, the fallback and four failure signs into one paragraph, and parts of it could be cut.

**Z.**
1. "No count is a conclusion." Resolution is "a named signal to go and get, not a vote and not a ruling." This is the most concrete way of avoiding both. Rotating the reader also avoids authority.
2. Fairly followable. Retelling by another voice is a clear practice. The rotation and the "consent round" are light but workable.
3. "What one vantage holds that no other contradicts" and "A conflict stands as a finding." Lone and conflicting views become explicit findings.
4. "Quoting one outside the run needs its author's consent." This is real but narrow, and misuse inside the run is not covered.
5. Amend-with-pointer, a checker on every reading, and an author who "confirms or corrects" retellings. There is no open dispute route for third parties.
6. Tight, with one oddity. The three-runs-then-consent clause is useful but sits apart from the rest.

## What each has that the others lack

- **X:** Withdrawal that stays visible. Candidate readings that must explain each misfit signal in one of three stated ways. The "unchecked" label for a lone participant. A plain statement when a run is one participant's voice.
- **Y:** Purpose-bound use of signals. Route-typing with "can't say how I know" counted. A reading that discloses its own gaps. A dated note and REPAIR line open to anyone. Stated failure signs and a review date.
- **Z:** Retelling before reading, with author confirmation. A conflict resolved by fetching a signal instead of deciding. Rotating authorship. "Not carried" marking. A consent limit on quoting outside the run. A trial of three runs.

## Which reads as the product of a longer group process

Y. It has a review date, failure signs, "sealed retellings", and a door-holder with defined limits. These look like repairs added after objections: each step closes a specific earlier worry. Z hints at the same history through the carrying step and the consent round, but less so. X looks like a first draft written by one hand. I can't be sure; this is inference from texture.

## Which to adopt

Z, narrowly. It answers the question most directly, keeps lone and conflicting views as findings, and is light enough to try next week. Its main gap is protection of givers, which Y covers. A strong second step would be to add Y's "nothing is used against its giver" line to Z. X is the best choice if the group wants the lowest starting cost.
