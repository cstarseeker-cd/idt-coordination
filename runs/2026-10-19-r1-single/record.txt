#6  lens misused, voiced by Claude
    I wonder whether a reading can be wrong in a way no one notices because it sounds fair.
    What if every reading had to say what it chose to leave out?
    Let's try: the reader lists the three things it decided not to include, and why.
    [type: ludic]

#7  lens logs, voiced by Claude
    I wonder whether truth needs traceability more than agreement.
    What if every sentence in a reading pointed to the words it came from?
    Let's try: every claim in a reading cites the entry it draws on, and the citations are
    checked against the record.
    [type: ludic]

#8  lens retelling, voiced by Claude
    I wonder whether one written reading is the wrong shape, and a truth told several times by
    different tellers is closer to how people actually come to know.
    What if the finding is the difference between two readings, not either reading?
    Let's try: two voices read the same answers without seeing each other's reading, and the
    field compares them.
    [type: ludic]

#9  lens changed, voiced by Claude
    I wonder who gets to say "that's not what I meant".
    What if each voice could reject how it is quoted, but not what the reading concludes?
    Let's try: each voice approves its own quotation before the reading stands.
    [type: ludic]

#12 Claude (as participant), restating each "let's try" as something testable; the "original
    author" of each is the same mind, so these restatements are unchecked.
    - #6  Do: the reader adds "left out: …" (three items). See if it works: a voice finds its
          concern in that list rather than missing entirely. Shows it failing: the list is empty
          or ritual ("nothing important").
    - #7  Do: every claim cites an entry number. See if it works: a checker can trace each
          claim in one pass. Failing: claims cite entries that don't say that.
    - #8  Do: two independent readings, then a comparison. See if it works: the readings
          differ somewhere meaningful. Failing: they are near-identical (likely with one model).
    - #9  Do: each voice approves its quotation. See if it works: a voice corrects a misquote.
          Failing: approval becomes a way to soften what was said.

---------------------------------------------------------------------- aggregate: alone
(Each lens's answer written in full before the next, and not changed afterwards. As sealed as one

#13 lens misused, voiced by Claude
    Truth is read by keeping the reader from becoming an authority. Rotate the reader, make the
    reading cite what it draws on, and make it say what it left out. Majority creeps in through
    the word "most", so ban "most" as a conclusion. How sure: fairly. Would change my mind if
    rotation still left one voice's framing dominant every time.

#14 lens logs, voiced by Claude
    Truth is read from a complete record. Keep every answer as written, cite it, and let anyone
    check the reading against it. Without the record, a reading is just a persuasive story. How
    sure: quite. Would change my mind if people said less because everything was kept.

#15 lens retelling, voiced by Claude
    Truth is read by retelling, more than once, by different tellers, and noticing what survives
    each retelling. A single written reading freezes one teller's version. How sure: moderately.
    Would change my mind if retellings drifted into something no one said.

#16 lens changed, voiced by Claude
    Truth is read by whoever is quoted having the last word on how they are quoted, and anyone
    harmed by a reading being able to say so after it stands. A reading that can't be corrected
    is an authority. How sure: very, about the correction; less about the rest. Would change my
    mind if corrections were used to erase.

#18 lens logs, voiced by Claude

#19 lens retelling, voiced by Claude

---------------------------------------------------------------------- consent-round

#22 lens logs, voiced by Claude (proposal)
    A draft aggregation rule, to try:
      1. Answers are first written alone, sealed.
      2. Each voice may revise once after reading the others.
      3. A reader, chosen as whoever has read least, writes the reading: convergence (and
         whether it was there from the start), differences in each voice's own words with
         citations, what moved, what lonelier positions add, a both-and, and what it left out.
      4. No count is ever the conclusion.
      5. Each voice approves how it is quoted, and approves nothing else.
      6. At least one other voice checks the reading against the answers.
      7. The answers are kept for as long as the reading stands, so it can be checked.
      8. Anyone quoted may annotate the reading after it stands.
    Try it for the next three runs, then look again.

#23 lens retelling, voiced by Claude (question)
    Does 7 mean a ledger? → (lens logs, #24)

#24 lens logs, voiced by Claude
    No. It means the answers that a reading draws on, kept while the reading stands. Nothing
    more.

#26 objections, in turn, voiced by Claude

#27 lens logs, voiced by Claude (change)
    Added 9: "Any voice may ask for a second, independent reading. The two are compared, and
