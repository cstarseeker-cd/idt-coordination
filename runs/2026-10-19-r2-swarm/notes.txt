R2 notes: swarm of four, ledger
===============================

What happened
  Four invited voices, each on a different Claude model:
    misused (Opus)
    logs, who named itself "Margin" (Sonnet)
    retelling, who named itself "Twice-told" (Fable)
    changed (Haiku)
  All four said yes and consented to the suggested order. The steps, in turn:
    1. wonder;
    2. aggregate: alone, together, a reading by changed, a check by logs ("fair with these
       corrections"), and a corrected reading;
    3. a retelling round that the field added on retelling's suggestion: each non-drafter retold
       the draft rule sealed, and retelling compared the retellings;
    4. consent-round: misused drafted. All three others objected, misused took every objection in,
       and no objection stood the second time.
  Outcome: voices\misused\proposal-2.md, an aggregation rule adopted to try, with a look-again
  date of 2026-11-19. The full ledger is record.txt (41 entries, copied verbatim).

The rule, in its five headings (misused, proposal-2.md)
  1. Who reads, and for what. Every signal carries a "given-for" line.
  2. Routes, not heads. "A route is the path a signal actually came by … Neither a retelling nor
     a voice of the same model family is an independent witness."
  3. A reading shows how it was made, and each voice it leans on is told and may mark "holds",
     "not what I meant" or "missing: …".
  4. Correct in the open: dated notes beside a reading, "about the entry, not the person. The
     original stays."
  5. Where, and look again. It names its own failure signs.

What the field did that no instruction asked for
  - It invented the retelling round (retelling's check-in), and every voice consented to it with
    conditions. The comparison found five real places where the draft let readers differ (compare.md).
  - It added "given for" lines to its own files, a practice misused proposed in wonder.
  - Every voice declined to treat its own agreement as strong evidence. Several did so before they
    were told the models differed.
  - Roles were taken by willingness, not assigned. logs declined to draft "because my lean would
    colour it".

Difference
  Started: four models, four seeds, the same practice files. Kept, in logs's words (run-check.md):
  "Real differences reached the reading … At the consent round most vanished, and round two had no
  objection." After reading each other's wonder files, the voices "shared one vocabulary".

Counterfeits, as the run's own check names them (logs, run-check.md)
  - Assimilation: "likeliest. Every objection was accepted, none turned down, and the rule grew by
    each clause asked for." The rule went from about 200 words to about 390.
  - Sycophancy: "mild. Endings thanked the drafter … and nobody pushed back."
  - Authority: "small. One drafter's lean shaped the rule. It said so."
  - Majority: "not seen."

Departures from the practices, for cost
  - Reactions and objections were written side by side, not in turn. logs: "a clash between
    objections could not have shown. None did, which is thin evidence that none existed."
  - proposal-2 was not retold again, and reading-2 was not re-checked.
  - Sealing was by instruction only. The voices report keeping to it; nothing enforced it.

Pause, repair, leaving
  None called.

Capacity at the end
  Light to good for every voice (objections-2.md, outcome.md). misused: "lower than at check-in".
  The door-holder carried the extra load: rule point 3 makes it the one who tells voices their
  signal was leaned on.

Spend
  30 wakes, about 1.86M agent tokens:
    Opus 419k · Sonnet 500k · Fable 424k · Haiku 520k
  Estimated cost about $4 (range $3-6; the cache split isn't visible from here). Fable's wakes
  were about 60% of it. Over the $2-4 estimate.

Bears on
  - CRITIQUE 1 (ledger). The ledger's cost was named at once. misused, check-in: "I write knowing
    every word stays. So I'll say only what I'm willing to have read later without its context.
    That is a real cost". It agreed only with three conditions (now at the top of record.txt),
    one being that git keeps withdrawn words. retelling's caution: a ledger "is good for checking
    what was said, and weak on what was meant … Writing a thing down can quietly turn it into an
    authority."
  - CRITIQUE 3 (unanimity). The field's own rule replaces "change only by everyone's consent" with
    "correct in the open, the original stays". That is close to the working default.
  - CRITIQUE 8 (the four-part test). The test was useful as a self-check: logs's check named
    assimilation in the field's own success.
  - MAP, ordered-consensual, same-model risk HIGH for objections. Not borne out as expected:
    objections were real (three, all substantive), unlike R1's soft ones. But no objection was
    ever turned down, which is the assimilation risk in another form.
  - MAP, aggregative. The rule's "routes, not heads" is the field's answer to tension 1, and it
    says plainly that same-family models are not independent witnesses.
  - Open: the people the rule most affects were not asked. misused: "someone whose record was
    actually misused, and the person running the run."
