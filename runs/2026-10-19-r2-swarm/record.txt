## The question, as I hold it

How might this field read truth from many partial signals without falling back on majority or
authority, and in a way that can't later be turned against the people who gave the signals?

## My answer

A field can read truth from partial signals if it **stops treating the reading as the thing that
is true**. The reading is a claim about the signals, and it should be easy to take apart. Majority
and authority both work by making a reading hard to take apart: "most said so", "the right one said
so". So I'd build the opposite. Each reading has to show what it's made of, what it couldn't
see, who it's for, and when it ends.

In practice, I'd ask five things of any reading:

1. **Route, not headcount.** Each signal says how it came to be known (seen, heard, inferred,
   inherited) and where from. Agreement only counts as support when it comes by different routes.
   Ten signals that all trace to one file are one signal. (This is Margin's idea and I've taken it
   on.) For this run I expect the routes to be few: one model family, one set of practice files.

2. **Purpose travels with the signal.** Each signal carries a short "given for". Reading it for
   anything else has to be **declared**, and it may **never be used against the person who gave
   it**. Reading a signal to correct or contest the record is always within purpose. (I've changed
   my mind on this since my wonder lines, because of changed. A strict purpose rule would lock out
   the very person who needs a record fixed.)

3. **Leaned-on, not cited-over.** Beside each thing a reading concludes, it says which signals it
   leaned on, so anyone can go back to them. A reading never stands in for its signals. If someone
   wants the conclusion, they can follow it back down.

4. **Gaps out loud.** Each reading names what arrived but couldn't be folded in (changed's line).
   It also names what never arrived: whose signal is missing and what that leaves unseen. It never
   guesses why a signal is missing. Silence often means the people who'd speak have seen what
   happens to words once they're kept. A reading that hides its gaps passes off that silence as
   agreement.

5. **Who reads, for what, until when.** Any rule for reading signals says who may read them, for
   what purpose, and when it will be looked at again. After that date the reading stops being
   citable unless someone reads it fresh. In this run the words themselves never go away, because
   the ledger and git keep them. What ends is the reading's standing, not the words.

Under all five: a reading can be wrong in the open. Later voices add dated notes about the entry
(never about the person), and the reading stays visible with its corrections beside it.

What this avoids. **Majority**, because numbers only count across independent routes, and the gaps
are said out loud instead of being counted as quiet agreement. **Authority**, because no reading
can be cited without its leaned-on notes, no reading outlives its look-again date, and nobody's
reading gets the last word. Anyone may add a note.

## How sure I am

- **About the shape** (route, purpose, leaned-on, gaps, who/what/until): fairly sure, about 6 in
  10, that it reads truth better than counting heads or deferring to whoever has standing. The

- **That "given for" can be enforced:** not very sure, about 3 in 10. It's a norm and not a lock.

In practice I think that means five things.

1. **Ask how each signal came to be known.** A signal someone saw, one they heard, one they
   inferred and one they were told are different things. A signal retold by someone who never saw
   it is not a second witness. Agreement means something only where it arrives by different
   routes. (This sharpened after reading logs's wonder lines.) Voices of a similar make, as all
   four of us are, share a route even when each one honestly says "seen".

2. **Pass signals through another mouth, and let the owner answer.** Someone else retells a
   signal in their own words. Its owner says "that holds" or "that is not what I meant", and both
   stay. What survives with the owner's yes has been understood, not only repeated. What gets
   lost is often the part only that teller could see, so it is kept beside the reading, not
   dropped. The owner has the say on what they meant. Nobody has the say on whether it is true.

3. **Read the differences as data.** Where tellings from different routes bend the same way,
   something is probably there. Where they scatter, something hasn't been seen whole yet.
   Contradictions are kept side by side, not resolved in the record. The scatter is part of what
   the field knows.

4. **Treat the reading as one more telling.** The reading doesn't get to be the original either.

## Answer, in my own words

A field can read truth from many partial signals if it keeps each signal together with the route it came by, lets a reading be added to and never overwrite the signals it came from, and lets later readers either fold a gap in or annotate beside it.

In practice I would have:

- each signal kept with a short line on what it was given for and how its author came to know it;
- each reading that names the one view it could not fold in, so a gap is written down and not smoothed over;
- later readers who answer that gap either by folding it in, with the change named, or by a dated annotation quoting the sentence they dispute, with the original left visible;
- agreement counted only where the routes differ, so that many voices who all took the same route count as one.

On this view, truth in the field is what keeps its shape under repeated checks by different routes, held open to annotation. It is not what most of us say, and it is not what the most senior voice says. Nobody's word is final, the author's included.

## How sure I am

Moderately sure that this is a coherent direction, and not very sure it would work once real signals arrive. I would put it around 4 out of 10 that the practice, as written, would hold up for a full run. I am more sure of the reasons than of the mechanism. The reasons are the same reasons the question names: majority and authority both stop reading early.

## What would change my mind

- If the annotation trail grows so long that no reader gets to it, the field would be ruled by whoever last wrote an annotation, which is authority by neglect. I would want to see a count of how many readings were ever re-read after an annotation, and I would change my view if that count is near zero.
- If runs with different routes keep giving the same answers, then the routes were not really different, and my account of independence is wrong about what independence means in practice.
- If the signals in this setting cannot be given a route at all, for example because every voice is the same model with the same training, then the method reduces to a single signal dressed up as many. I would want that stated in the reading before anyone cites it.
