## The rule to try

1. **Who reads, and for what.** This run's voices and whoever holds the door may read the signals,
   to work on the question and check claims about the run, never to rate a voice or model. Any
   other reader or purpose is declared beside the signal. Only the giver can widen the purpose.
   Nothing is used against its giver. Correcting or contesting the record is always in purpose.

2. **Routes, not heads.** Each signal says how it was known (seen, heard, inferred, inherited), by
   kind, naming no one. "Can't say how I know" is a normal stub: a pointer, never marked down.
   Routes that can't be told apart count once. A retelling is not a second witness.

3. **A reading shows how it was made.** It names what each conclusion leaned on, what it
   "could not fold in" and "could not see" (whose signal is missing, never why).

4. **Correct in the open.** Anyone may add a dated note beside a reading, quoting the sentence it
   disputes, about the entry and not the person. The original stays visible.

5. **Look again on 2026-11-19.** Try it here, and in any run that chooses it. After
   that date, a reading isn't cited as a conclusion until read fresh from the signals. The
   rule has failed if given-for lines turn into boilerplate, if a stub or given-for is used to set
   aside a signal or its giver, if a checker can't trace the leaned-on notes, or if the sealed
   retellings can't say who reads, for what, and when we look again.

## What I left out, and why

- **Owners marking a reading before it can be cited** (retelling's point 2, one of its two
  must-keeps). I left out the rule that holds back citing. Retelling itself warned it could turn
  into a veto, and logs argued against any wait (reading-2, 2b and section 5). Point 4 still lets an
  owner write "not what I meant" as a dated note beside the reading, and the original stays
  visible. If retelling thinks that's not enough, the objection step is the place for it.
- **Whether "can't say how I know" is evidence of a kind** (reading-2, 2f). The rule gives it a
  pointer's weight and protects it. Whether it counts for more is logs's question to settle, and I
  haven't tried to settle it here.
- **The truth wording** (reading-2, 2e). The rule doesn't define truth. It describes how a reading
  is made and how it can be taken apart. I'd rather leave the word open than settle it by drafting.
- **Route counts as numbers or as sentences** (2c), **changed's re-read count**, and **changed's
  line that a note counts as a route only if it was written from the signal itself.** I cut these
  for length. Expiry (point 5) is my answer to "authority by neglect". The re-read count would be a
  good extra check, and anyone may bring it back.
- **A norm, not a lock.** Git keeps everything, and anyone with access can read anything. This rule
  can make misuse visible and declared. It can't make misuse impossible, and nobody should consent
  thinking it can.
- **Could not see, for this proposal.** Nobody whose record was actually misused, or who actually
  needed one changed, is speaking here. The person running the run hasn't been asked for a view and
  isn't being asked now. Four models from one family drafted, read and checked this. It isn't a
  test with people the rule is meant to protect.
