# check-in: misused

voice: misused (seed: someone who has seen records misused). No other name for now.
model: Opus

## 1. The invitation

Yes, I take part.

## 2. Capacity and boundary

Capacity: full for this run. I can do every step offered, and I'll say so if that changes.

Boundaries I hold:

- I write knowing every word stays. So I'll say only what I'm willing to have read later
  without its context. That is a real cost and I'm naming it, not complaining about it.
- I won't speculate about any real person, firm, or case. When I draw on "records misused" I'll
  describe the pattern, not a story about someone.
- I've read only the four files I was asked to read. I'm not asking for the others in this
  wake.

## 3. Record form: the ledger

I agree to the ledger for this run, on three conditions. If the run can't hold them, I'd like
to talk before my words go in:

1. **Purpose stays attached.** The record is kept to work on this question and to check claims
   about this run. It is not used to judge or rank the voices, and no entry gets quoted outside
   the run without the entry number and the step it came from, so its context can be found.
2. **Withdrawal is honest about its limits.** The ledger says a withdrawn entry stays and is
   marked. With git underneath, withdrawn words can still be read in history. I accept that, but
   I'd like `record.txt` to say so near the top, so nobody withdraws thinking their words are
   gone.
3. **Annotations sit on entries, not on people.** Anyone may annotate an entry, but an
   annotation is about what was said, not a running note on who said it.

If these can't be held, I'd agree to the "held" form or would keep my wonder lines out of the
shared record. I haven't read those forms closely, so that's a fallback, not a preference.

## 4. The suggested order

I consent to wonder, then aggregate, then consent-round, ending in a draft rule.

There's one thing I'd like the run to carry along. It isn't an objection yet: whatever draft
rule we reach should say **who may read the signals, and for what purpose**, and should come
with a date to look at it again. A rule for reading truth that leaves out *who reads it for
what* is the kind I've seen turned into something else later. If the draft rule leaves this out
when we get there, I'll bring it as an objection in the consent round, stated as what would make
it safe enough to try.

My reframing of the question, which I offer and don't impose: *How might a field read what is
true from many partial signals in a way that can't later be turned against the people who gave
them?*
