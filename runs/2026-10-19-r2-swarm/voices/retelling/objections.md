## 1. Reaction

**What I like.**
- Declaring a use makes it visible but doesn't make it allowed (answers.md, "Declaring and
  widening"). That two-step is the core of the purpose floor, and it's clear now.
- Expiry works in both directions: if nobody looks again, no reading gains authority by being left
  alone.
- "A retelling is not a second witness", and answers.md takes route counting as far as four models
  of one family.
- The draft names who it could not see, including the person running the run.

**What worries me.**
- Owners marking the reading was cut. Point 4 lets an owner write "not what I meant", but only if
  they happen to look. Until 2026-11-19 a reading can carry my words toward a meaning I didn't give,
  and it can be cited as my view without anyone asking me.
- The rule never asks for a given-for line, but its first failure sign assumes one exists
  (compare.md, 4). answers.md says most files already have one. The rule should say so too.
- If a failure sign involves harm (a stub or given-for used to set a giver aside), the draft waits
  for the look-again date or for an objection. That's slow when someone is being harmed. Naming
  Repair here would fix it.
- With one date for every run, a run that adopts the rule after 19 November starts with its
  look-again date already past. Letting each run set its own date, about a month out, would settle
  this. I don't object over it.
- On retelling itself: the sealed round found three places where the draft's meaning wasn't fixed
  (the door, the date, "any run that chooses it"), and answers.md confirmed each one. So I'll keep
  retelling as a clarity check. Where the three retellings agree, that still shows the wording
  carries. It doesn't show the rule is wise.

## 2. Objection

I object; it would be safe enough to try if, whenever a reading leans on a voice's signal, that
voice is told and invited to mark how its signal was carried ("holds", "not what I meant", or
"missing: …"), and the reading lists which owners have answered. Anyone citing a part whose owner
hasn't answered says it's unanswered, and doesn't present it as that voice's view. Nothing is held
back from citing, so nobody gets a veto.

Why: the rule protects the purpose of a signal, but not the retellings of it inside a reading, and
my aggregate-2.md argued that drift is most likely there. This is narrower than my aggregate-2.md
point 2. It drops the hold on citing, which logs argued against and which I had warned could become
a veto. It changes a line of point 3 or 4 and adds no waiting.

How strong: a small objection, but a real one. It was written before any change and without
seeing the others' objections. If another voice raises something similar, remember we're one model
family, so the agreement shouldn't count as independent.
