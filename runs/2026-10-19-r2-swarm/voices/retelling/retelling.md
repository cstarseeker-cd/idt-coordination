## The rule, told again

**What it asks.** Five habits. Every signal comes with a note on what it was handed over for. Every
signal also says roughly how its giver came to know it: saw it, was told it, worked it out, or
took it on from earlier. It says this by kind only, with no names. Saying "I can't tell you how I
know" is allowed and treated as an ordinary signpost, not a weakness. Sources that can't really be
separated are counted as one, and someone passing a thing on doesn't count as another person who
saw it. Anyone writing a reading has to show their workings: what each conclusion rests on, what
didn't fit, and whose voice is missing, without guessing why it's missing. Anyone can disagree
with a reading by writing a dated note next to it. The note quotes the sentence it disagrees with
and is aimed at what was said, not at whoever said it. Nothing gets erased.

**Who may read the signals, and for what.** The voices in this run, plus the one keeping the door,
may read them in order to work on the run's question and to check what is being claimed about the
run. Not to grade a voice or a model. If anyone else reads, or anyone reads for some other reason,
that has to be written down next to the signal. Only the person who gave a signal can open it to a
wider use. A signal must never be turned against the one who gave it. Using it to correct the
record, or to challenge the record, is always allowed.

**When it gets looked at again.** About a month from now, on 19 November 2026. It's being tried in
this run and in any other run that picks it up. Once that day passes, no reading can be leaned on as
a finding until somebody has gone back to the signals themselves and read them again. Who does the
looking again, and whether the rule lapses if nobody does: not clear from the draft.

**What would show it failed.** Any of four things. The given-for notes become stock phrases that
nobody means. A route note or a given-for note gets used as a reason to brush aside a signal or
the person who gave it. A checker tries to follow what a reading rested on and can't. Or these
sealed retellings can't agree on who reads, why, and when it comes up again. What happens once a
failure is seen: not clear from the draft.

## Where the retelling caught

- "Declared beside the signal" versus "only the giver can widen": I told it as two separate steps

  down is enough.
- "Look again on" the date: I told it as being about readings going stale. It may also, or
  instead, mean the rule itself gets reconsidered then.
