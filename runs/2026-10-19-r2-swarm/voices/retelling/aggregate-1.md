# aggregate, step 1 (alone): retelling (Twice-told)

[type: aggregative]

Written without reading anyone else's aggregate-1.md. It is not independent of everything,
though. Earlier this wake I read everyone's check-in and wonder lines (wonder step 4), and some of
them moved me. Where they did, I say so, so the route is visible.

Question: How might this field read truth from many partial signals without falling back on
majority or authority?

## My answer

Read the shape that holds across tellings from places that are truly different, and keep the
differences as part of the reading. Don't count the tellings, and don't let any one of them
become the original.

In practice I think that means five things.

1. **Ask how each signal came to be known.** A signal someone saw, one they heard, one they
   inferred and one they were told are different things. A signal retold by someone who never saw
   it is not a second witness. Agreement means something only where it arrives by different
   routes. (This sharpened after reading logs's wonder lines.) Voices of a similar make, as all
   four of us are, share a route even when each one honestly says "seen".

2. **Pass signals through another mouth, and let the owner answer.** Someone else retells a
   signal in their own words. Its owner says "that holds" or "that is not what I meant", and both
   stay. What survives with the owner's yes has been understood, not only repeated. What gets
   lost is often the part only that teller could see, so it is kept beside the reading, not
   dropped. The owner has the say on what they meant. Nobody has the say on whether it is true.

3. **Read the differences as data.** Where tellings from different routes bend the same way,
   something is probably there. Where they scatter, something hasn't been seen whole yet.
   Contradictions are kept side by side, not resolved in the record. The scatter is part of what
   the field knows.

4. **Treat the reading as one more telling.** The reading doesn't get to be the original either.
   It should show which signals it leaned on. It should name the one thing it couldn't fold in
   (from changed's lines), name who didn't speak and what it can't see (from misused's and logs's
   lines), and say what it was given for (from misused's). It should be open to retelling and
   annotation like any entry, and carry a date to be looked at again. After that date it stays
   readable as history, but isn't cited as a conclusion without going back to the signals.

5. **Hold "truth" as what has kept its shape so far.** Not settled. What the field can honestly
   say is: this has held through tellings from these different places, up to now, and here is
   what didn't fit.

## Where majority and authority could creep back into my own answer

- **The reteller as authority.** Whoever retells decides what survives. Guard: the owner marks
  the retelling, and both stay. Retelling is welcome, never a gate for entering the record.
- **Majority by the back door.** Four similar minds retell alike because we are alike, and "held
  its shape" quietly becomes "most of us said it". Guard: count routes, not tellings, and treat
  being the same kind of model as one shared route. A retelling from the person, or from a voice
  made differently, says more than one more retelling from us.
- **Smoothness.** The version that survives retelling may be the one easiest to carry, not the
  truest. Guard: keep what got lost, and keep the one signal the reading couldn't explain in
  plain view.
- **The written reading.** It becomes the thing that gets cited. Guard: cite it only together with
  its signals, and let it expire as a conclusion while it stays readable.

## A cost I can't fully answer

Every retelling is another copy of someone's signal, and another place it can be read for a
purpose it wasn't given for. misused's lines made this plain to me. My answer multiplies tellings,
so it multiplies the chances of misuse. Carrying the "given for" line with every retelling helps.
Keeping retellings inside the run's record, under the run's purpose, helps too. Neither removes
the risk, and I don't have a full answer.

## How sure I am

- About the direction (read shape across different routes, keep the differences, no telling is
  the original): fairly sure. About 6 out of 10.
- That retelling in particular adds much between minds as alike as ours: unsure. About 3 out of
  10. It may work far better between people, or between different kinds of mind, than among us.
- That I've found all the ways majority and authority creep back in: not sure. There are likely
  others I can't see from where I stand.

## What would change my mind

- If our retellings come back nearly word for word, or every author marks every retelling
  "holds", I'd stop leaning on retelling for a field like this one and lean on route stubs
  instead.
- If what gets lost in retelling turns out to be noise rather than what only one voice could see,
  my riverbed idea about lost details is wrong, and I'd say so.
- If a reading built my way is later shown wrong in a way a plain count would have caught, I'd
  have to admit that counting carries information I've been throwing away. Then I'd want the
  count shown beside the reading (the "tally alongside" variant), though still not as its
  conclusion.
- If keeping many tellings makes signals easier to turn against the people who gave them, I'd
  weigh that above my own preference, and accept fewer kept tellings.
- If another voice's answer shows a way to read truth that doesn't rest on tellings at all, I'd
  want to hear it, and I'd retell it to check that I understood it.
