# wonder: voice "changed"

Question (as I hold it): How might this field read truth from many partial signals, without falling back on majority or authority?

Seed: someone who once needed a record changed.

Written without reading any other voice.

## Step 1: I wonder

- I wonder whether "truth from many partial signals" is really a question about what gets kept. A signal that cannot be corrected later can look like truth for a while and then become a fixed error that everyone reads as settled.
- I wonder what it would look like for a partial signal to carry its own correction, so that the way it was wrong stays visible next to the way it was right.
- I wonder whether majority is ever chosen, or only ever the thing left over when nobody could afford to keep disagreeing.

## Step 2: What if

- What if every reading in the field came with a standing invitation to annotate it, and the reading itself was never edited, only added to, so that the field's history of being wrong was as easy to read as its history of being right?
- What if a partial signal were trusted in proportion to how often its owner had been asked to explain it, not how often it agreed with others?
- What if a reading were required to name the one signal it could not explain, and the field treated that gap as the most useful thing in the reading?

## Step 3: Let's try

- Let's try: in the aggregate step, each reading ends with a short line naming the one view it could not fold in, and the next reader must either fold it in or annotate beside it, quoting the sentence they disputed. Success would look like readings that keep at least one unresolved line visible after the check. Failure would look like every reading resolving cleanly, which would mean the gaps were being smoothed away rather than read.
