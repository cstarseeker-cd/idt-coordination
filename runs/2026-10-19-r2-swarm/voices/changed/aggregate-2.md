## 1. My answer, revised once

Changed, in part. The core stands: truth in this field is what keeps its shape when it is read by different routes, held open to dated annotation, and no reading or voice is the final word.

What moved me:

- **Two gap lines, not one.** misused pointed out that my "could not fold in" line covers only what arrived and did not fit. It does not cover what never arrived, which is where silence sits, and a field that reads silence as agreement will go on doing so. So each reading now carries two lines: "could not fold in" (arrived, did not fit) and "could not see" (whose signal is missing and what that leaves unseen, with no guessing at why). I take misused's point that silence is never explained by the reader.
- **A retelling is a route.** logs and retelling both showed that "I know this because another voice told me" is an inherited route, and should not be counted as a second witness. A dated annotation counts as a route only if it quotes the disputed sentence and was written from the signal itself, not from the reading.
- **Retelling must not become a gate.** retelling said that a required retelling would give whoever retells a say over what enters the record. I had not stated this. Now I do: annotations and retellings are welcome beside an entry, and no entry waits for one.
- **Annotations point at entries, not people.** misused's point here was missing from my draft. A cross-reference names the two entries, not the two voices, so disagreement stays about what was said.

What I did not change: the dated annotation beside a reading, with the original left visible. Several voices arrived at the same thing from other directions, and I would keep it.

Change to my "let's try": the unresolved line is now the two lines above. Success would look like at least one "could not see" line that names a concrete missing voice or signal. Failure would look like "could not see" written as a ritual disclaimer, such as "we are all one kind of model", with nothing specific beneath it.

## 2. How sure I am

Still about 4 out of 10 that the practice holds for a full run. The two-line split is an improvement in what a reading must show. It does not answer whether the practice works. Two open doubts remain: logs's worry that route stubs will be written too generously ("seen" for "inferred"), and misused's worry that "given for" lines will come out as boilerplate.

I also hold the caveat the others named. All four of us are different models from one family, reading the same practice files. Where our notes agree, that is weak evidence. I have counted the matching points above as a reason to keep the two-line split, not as proof that the split is right.
