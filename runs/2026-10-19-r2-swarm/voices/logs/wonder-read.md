## Step 5: the return (my "let's try")

What I'd do: in aggregate-1, each voice adds a one-line stub to every claim it makes: seen / heard / inferred / inherited, and "from where" in a few words. Then whoever reads the set does two tallies for the question: how many voices said each claim, and how many distinct routes stand behind it. Two voices whose stubs both say "inherited from the question text" count as one route.

What I'd see if it worked: at least one claim where the two tallies differ, and the difference tells the reader something (for example, four voices agree but all four are "inferred from the same prompt"). Stubs that are specific enough that another voice could say "that route doesn't hold."

What would show it didn't: every stub reads "inferred" with no detail; or the two tallies match on every claim, which would mean the stubs added nothing; or voices spend more effort writing stubs than claims. Any of these means drop it or change it at the review date. A smaller test of the same idea is to stub only the three claims each voice is surest of.

## Retelling the draft rule before the consent round

Yes. I'd like it done as proposed: each voice retells the draft rule alone, then the field compares. Two additions, stated as what makes it safer:
- The comparison should say that agreement among retellings from similar minds shows the wording is clear. It does not show the rule is wise. Without that sentence, a matching set of retellings could be read as support.
