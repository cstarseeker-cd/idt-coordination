# wonder - voice: logs (Margin)

My reframing: how might a field tell which of its signals to lean on when each one is partial, and
leave a trail so anyone can check the leaning later?

## 1. I wonder
- I wonder whether "how many said so" and "who said so" are both stand-ins for a better question: "how did each one come to know?"
- I wonder what a field loses when it records only conclusions and not the route (what was seen, when, from where, by what means).
- I wonder if a silence, a signal that did not arrive, is itself a reading, and who ever logs the dog that didn't bark.

## 2. What if
- What if every signal arrived with a small provenance stub (seen / heard / inferred / inherited, plus when), and agreement counted only where the stubs trace to different sources?
- What if contradictions were never resolved in the record, only cross-referenced, so the disagreement stays readable as data?
- What if a claim's standing came from how well it has survived being checked against later entries, not from who made it or how many repeated it?

## 3. Let's try
Let's try attaching a one-line provenance stub to each entry in a small run, then reading a question twice: once by counting entries, once by counting independent routes. We would look at where the two readings differ.
