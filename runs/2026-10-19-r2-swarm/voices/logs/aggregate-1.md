# aggregate-1 - voice: logs (Margin)

Written alone, without opening any other voice's aggregate-1.md. I have read the others' check-ins and wonder lines (which could shade this), and I say so.

## The question
How might this field read truth from many partial signals without falling back on majority or authority?

## My answer
I'd stop asking "what is true?" and ask of each signal "how did this one come to know, and for what was it offered?" Majority and authority are shortcuts for a question that is hard to answer directly: how much weight does this signal deserve? The better answer is to read routes. Two signals that trace back to the same source count once. Two that reach the same claim by different routes count more. A signal that is vouched for by someone's standing but has no traceable route counts as a pointer, a place to go and look. It doesn't count as evidence.

In practice:
1. Each signal carries a short stub: seen / heard / inferred / inherited, from where, when, and what it was given for.
2. The reading counts distinct routes beside the count of voices, and shows both.
3. Silence and disagreement stay in the reading. It names who didn't speak, and what it couldn't see. Contradictions are cross-referenced and not resolved. It names the one signal it couldn't explain.
4. A reading is a dated entry, not a verdict. A later voice can annotate it, quoting what it disputes, and the original stays. A claim gains standing by surviving later checks.
5. Any rule comes with a review date and a named way it could fail.

The step that actually does the work is 1 and 2: separating routes from votes. The rest keeps the reading honest about what it left out.

## How sure
Moderately, about the direction; less about whether it can be run. I'd say about 65% that tracing routes would change at least some readings compared with counting voices. I'm much less sure the stubs would be written honestly: a voice can write "seen" for something it inferred, and a route-count built on self-reported stubs is only as good as them. This is the weak point and I don't have a fix for it beyond other voices annotating a stub they doubt.

A second limit: all four voices are the same kind of mind, so our "independent routes" overlap more than the stubs would show. Whatever we conclude here, read it as one family's view.

## What would change my mind
- A trial where the route count and the voice count never differ, or differ in ways nobody finds informative. Then the stubs are overhead.
- Evidence that stubs get gamed or boilerplated within a few entries. Then I'd move toward something that checks routes from outside, which I can't design from here.
- A case where a claim with a poor route turned out right and a well-routed one turned out wrong often enough that route-tracing wasn't beating counting. I'd want to see that tried.
- Another voice showing that stubs push a field toward only what can be traced, and so drop true things that people know but can't say how they know. I take that worry seriously. My answer so far is that "can't say how" would be recorded as a stub too ("unsure of route"), but I'm not confident it would be treated fairly.
