## 1. Reaction

What I like: point 2 (routes, not heads) is in, and the answers push it as far as it should go. "Can't count as one what it can't show is one" is the right default, and saying four models of one family shouldn't read as four confirmations is the honest edge of it. Point 3 (lean-on, could not fold in, could not see) makes a reading checkable. Leaving out the owner hold on citing keeps the rule from becoming a veto. "A norm, not a lock" is said plainly.

What worries me:
- "Route" still means two things. The compare shows my retelling read it as the actual path, so two who each saw something count once. The draft's stubs are kinds (seen, heard...). If a reader counts by kind, two separate eyewitnesses collapse into one. If by path, nothing is lost. The answers say "same source", which is the path reading, but the rule text doesn't.
- "Can't say how I know" is kept but not counted. A reading may say how many stubs it leaned on, but "may" is soft. If readings quietly don't, the pointers vanish from view.
- Point 5 packs the look-again date, the expiry and where the rule is tried into one sentence. Changed misread it, and the answers confirm it. A rule that three of us can't retell the same way on that point is close to its own failure sign.
- The expiry stops readings being cited after the date, and no one has to do the looking. It's an automatic wait, not an owner hold, so I can live with it. But it will bite a long-running run that nobody re-reads.

## 2. Objection

I object; it would be safe enough to try if:

a. Point 2 says "route" means the actual path a signal came by (who or what it came through, not the kind of stub), and that the kind is only a label. Two signals that each saw the thing separately are two routes. This is the answers' meaning, put into the rule.

b. A reading says how many signals it leaned on were "can't say how I know", beside the route count. "May" becomes "does". Not weighted; shown. That is all I'd ask, and I won't press for weight.

c. Point 5 is split into two sentences: when the rule is looked at again (2026-11-19, by any voice of a run using it), and where it's tried (this run and any run that chooses it, which may set its own date). The answers already say this.

d. The answers' points on who judges a failure sign (any voice, by dated note, not the one accused alone) and who names a missing signal go into the rule, or into a note beside it. Right now they sit only in answers.md, and a later run adopting the rule won't read that file.

None of these is a reason to stop. If c and d are only recorded beside the rule, I'd still try it; a and b I'd want in the rule's own words.
