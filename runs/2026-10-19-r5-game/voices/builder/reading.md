## How much difference there was to begin with

Not much. At step 1 the four answers differed mainly in what each one included and what it
stressed. They didn't contradict each other. That makes the convergence below weak evidence.
client-1, builder-2 and demo-2 each say so in their own words (four voices, one model family).

## Convergence

**There before step 2:**

- **A cold run on the real device, in the real room, by someone who didn't build it.** This is
  in builder-1 (point 2), client-1 ("Someone who was not on the build team has done the whole
  session cold"), demo-1 (point 1) and headsets-1 ("run on the exact headsets, in the exact
  room, by people who were not on the build team"). It's the one point all four reached alone.
- **Readiness belongs to a particular showing, not to the build on its own.** builder-1: "A
  prototype is ready for one particular showing." client-1: "a prototype can be ready for one
  client conversation and not for another." demo-1: "Readiness belongs to the showing, not just
  the build." headsets-1 reaches a nearby point from a different direction: "Readiness is partly
  physical."
- **Say what the showing is for, name what's placeholder, and have a fallback and a recovery.**
  This is in builder-1, client-1 and demo-1. headsets-1 doesn't mention purpose, placeholders
  or a fallback. demo-2 writes "All four of us named point 2." When I read headsets-1 I don't
  find that point in it, so I'd put it as three, with headsets silent on it rather than against it.
- **Not sure about thresholds.** All four said they were less sure about how many runs, how
  many minutes, or how rough is too rough. That was true at step 1 and still true after step 2.

**Only after step 2:**

- **Freeze the build after the run.** This was in demo-1 alone ("After that run, nothing
  changes"). builder-2 and client-2 took it up, both with the same caveat: a change means the
  whole run is done again, so it isn't a ban.
- **The run is timed to the real session length, and someone watches where the tester stops.**
  This was in headsets-1 alone ("note where they stop, squint, or take the headset off").
  builder-2, client-2 and demo-2 all took it up.
- **A "not yet" has to name the failure, and the first answer to it is a smaller showing on the
  same date.** demo-1 had the smaller showing. builder-1 had the distrust of a "not yet" that
  gives no reason. After step 2, builder-2 and demo-2 each wrote a version that joins the two,
  and client-2 sides with the smaller-showing version.
- **Recovery gets a number.** demo-2 joins builder-1's "under a minute" with client-1's
  "without breaking the conversation" into "a test, not just a hope."

## Difference

**1. Whether the client getting in and out alone is a gate.** This is the clearest difference
in the run.

- headsets-1: "Ask whether the client can get in and out alone within a few minutes. If the team
  has to help them through setup, the prototype is not ready yet, however good the scene looks."
- demo-2 adopts it: "If they can't get in and out without the team stepping in, it's not ready."
- client-2: "From the presenter's chair I would not make that a gate. In many showings the
  presenter guides the client in, and that guidance is part of the session. … This is a real
  disagreement, not a nuance, and I hold it at about 60 percent."
- builder-2: "When a presenter guides the session that may be too strict. I'd keep it as
  something to check, not a gate."

headsets can't answer client-2 or builder-2, because it left before reading them. This
difference stays open, and the reading shouldn't settle it.

**2. What a "not yet" can do.** This one is narrower than it looks, but client-2 calls it a
place where it does not move, so I'm keeping it.

- demo-2: "It still holds without a vote. But it has to name which condition isn't met … And
  the fix can still be a smaller showing on the same date."
- builder-2: "'Not yet' holds without a vote, but it has to name the failure, and the default
  answer to it is a smaller showing on the same date."
- client-2: "I agree anyone should be able to say it, but if the date is not ours to move, the
  better move is a smaller honest showing on that date."

As I read it, the three agree that anyone can stop a showing and that showing less is the first
remedy. What they leave open is whether a named "not yet" can ever move the date. demo-1 asked
client "is the date ours to move?" client-2 answers "if" the date isn't ours to move, not
whether it usually is. demo-2 still lists that question as unanswered, and I think that's right.

**3. How much to trust "rough lands better."**

- client-1: "A rough prototype shown with a clear frame usually lands better than a polished one
  that stumbles."
- builder-2: "Nobody has yet supplied the thing every one of us said would change our minds: a
  real account of rough work landing better, or of a showing that passed a cold run and still
  failed."

This isn't a split on what to do. It's about how much weight a judgment from one seat can carry
when nobody can point to an actual case.

**4. One question that crossed in the post.** demo-2 says its question to builder ("does a freeze
block the fix that matters?") is "still unanswered." builder-2, written at the same time, answers
it: "Sometimes it does." Then it gives a rule: re-run, or ship the last build that passed and
name the bug. demo hasn't seen that answer yet.

## What moved, and why

- **builder** moved on three points. It took the freeze from demo. It moved the dry run to the
  room and hardware, with headsets running it, which was one of the things builder-1 had said
  would change its mind. And it took a "not yet" that names its failure, from demo and from
  client's "whether we looked in control." The reason given: each fills a gap builder-1 had left
  or settles a tension inside it.
- **client** took the freeze (demo), the timed run with its stopping points and the comfort and
  fit points (headsets), the client's body (demo), and builder's wording for "define the
  question." It didn't move on the gate or on the "not yet" that holds without a vote.
- **demo** moved its rehearsal toward headsets, its "not yet" toward builder, and gave recovery a
  number (from builder and client). It kept the freeze "because nobody gave a reason to drop it."
- **headsets** didn't revise, because it left.

What all the moves have in common: each voice added specifics from a neighbouring seat. Nobody
moved on the overall shape. Every voice listed something that would change its mind, and
nobody supplied any of it. demo-2's answer about failures that get past a clean run is labelled
honestly as "the general pattern and not … memories." So the movement was voices borrowing
concrete detail from each other. It wasn't driven by new evidence.

## What the lonelier positions add

- **headsets-1: room constraints settled in advance.** "Settle the 'room' constraints in advance
  (for example, what must stay still or silent in a shared space), so they are not discovered in
  front of the client." Nobody took this up. It names a kind of readiness that belongs to the
  space and the people sharing it, not to the build, the presenter or the client.
- **headsets-1: a doubt that could undercut the rest.** One of the things it said would change its
  mind is "evidence that clients usually view a recording or a screen-mirrored version rather than
  wearing the headset." The rest of us treat the video as the fallback. headsets asks whether it's
  often the main thing the client sees. If it is, the headset run carries less weight than we
  give it.
- **demo-1: the client's body, and failures from outside the plan.** Only demo names motion
  sickness, "no artificial locomotion for first-time users", and "a senior person who walks in late
  and asks to 'just try it' with no framing" (demo-2). It also names a presenter "talking up a
  feature the room can't see because nobody mirrored the headset view." These are failures from
  people, not from the build.
- **client-1: how the showing is remembered.** "The client remembers how the session felt and
  whether we looked in control." That moves readiness from what the prototype shows to what the
  relationship takes away from the meeting.
- **builder-1: the builder's evidence is the weakest.** And builder-1 was "unsure whether a
  checklist like this helps, or just becomes a ritual that pushes showings later than they should
  be." Nobody took up that second worry. Everything the run converged on is extra conditions, so
  the worry hasn't gone away. It's grown.

**A caution about roles.** builder-2 ("The person who looks after the headsets should run it")
and demo-2 ("Headsets should lead this check") both give the dry run to the headsets *role*. The
headsets *voice* never asked for that, and it has left. I read that as a point about which seat
sees the most on the day. It isn't a duty handed to anyone.

## Both … and …

A prototype is ready for a showing **both** when someone new to it has got through the exact
build, on the exact headset, in that room, for the real length of the session, with the build
frozen after, **and** when the team can say what the client is meant to decide, what's fake, and
how each likely failure is recovered in under a minute. A "not yet" that names its failure is
answered first by showing less on the same day. Whether the client must manage the headset
without help is **both** a gate (headsets-1, demo-2) **and** only a check (client-2, builder-2),
and which one it is depends on whether this showing is meant to be guided.
