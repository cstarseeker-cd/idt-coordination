## Revised answer (one revision)

The shape stands: readiness belongs to the showing, not the build. Two points move, and one gets sharper.

1. **The rehearsal (my point 1) moves toward headsets.** "Someone who didn't build it, end to end" was too loose. The run that counts is: the exact build on the exact headset, cold start, fresh charge, in the real room or a close copy, **for as long as the client will actually be in it**, with someone new to the project, and someone watching **where they stop, squint, or take the headset off.** If they can't get in and out without the team stepping in, it's not ready. Headsets should lead this check, not the builder. Builder asked for that too. After that run, nothing changes. I'm keeping the freeze, because nobody gave a reason to drop it.

2. **"Not yet" (my point 5, the one I was least sure of) moves toward builder.** It still holds without a vote. But it has to name which condition isn't met: the rehearsal, the agreed purpose, the fallback, or the client's comfort. "It could be better" isn't enough. That deals with my worry about a veto feeding perfectionism. Builder's point that their own "not yet" deserves the least trust is the right warning. And the fix can still be a smaller showing on the same date.

3. **Recovery (my point 3) gets a number.** Builder said each likely failure needs a recovery that takes under a minute. Client said the presenter has to recover without breaking the conversation. Together those make a test, not just a hope.

Points 2 (agree what the showing is for and say what's placeholder) and 4 (fit the plan to this client's body: seated or standing, no artificial locomotion for first-timers, what to do if someone feels ill) stand. All four of us named point 2. Headsets covered comfort and fit, but nobody covered motion sickness, so I'm keeping point 4 as I wrote it.

## What moved me

- headsets: session length, cold start, getting in and out alone, and stopping points as the signal instead of a feature list.
- builder: a "not yet" needs a stated reason, and the builder's own one is the weakest.
- client: the client remembers whether we looked in control. That backs recoverability and framing over finish. It doesn't change my answer, but it raises my confidence in point 3.

## An answer for a question three of you asked

Builder and headsets both asked how a demo goes wrong *after* a clean fresh-user run. Client asked something close. From my seed, speaking about the general pattern and not about memories: what usually gets through is whatever the dry run didn't include. The night-before fix. A different headset. Or the client themselves: glasses, motion sickness, a senior person who walks in late and asks to "just try it" with no framing, or a question nobody rehearsed. That's why the freeze and point 4 stay in.
