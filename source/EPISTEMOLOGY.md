# An Epistemology of Coordination

Typologies drawn from the *Atlas of Coordination*

Oct 14, 2026 · @Chance

Read as an epistemology, the *Atlas of Coordination* yields nine types of coordination, placed on four axes. Here, epistemology means how participants come to know things together, and how coordination depends on that knowing.

Each type has a counterfeit that imitates it, and the Atlas implies a four-part test that tells them apart. Five tensions in the text need resolving before the typology can be used. Most of them land on the covenant the Atlas leaves unwritten (§30).

Section numbers (§) and maxims refer to the Atlas as it stands on the `atlas-audit` branch. The maxims all sit in §29.

## The epistemology underneath

Five commitments carry the epistemic weight of the Atlas. Signal is its unit of knowledge, and noise is what remains when signal fails.

1. **Knowledge depends on disclosure.** Intention, capacity, boundaries and harm are private states. The field knows them only once they are broadcast.
   - The field is not clairvoyant (Maxim 7). Capacity can't be known unless expressed (§12). Boundaries count as signal (Maxim 3).
   - Silence produces vagueness and noise (§24), yet disclosure can never be compelled (§28).
2. **Truth comes from the aggregate.** It emerges when honest, partial, differing signals meet. It is not the majority's view or an authority's ruling (Maxim 4, §22).
3. **Certainty is a political instrument.** Claims to certainty are treated as bids for control rather than achievements (§7). Will, faith and story count as ways of knowing, and reason is held loosely (§7, §20).
4. **Knowing is relational and builds over time.** Trust grows with the number of interactions (§10). Consent is reached in layers (§28). Recursion is return with difference (Maxim 6).
5. **The check against delusion is a return to others.** Will developed only alone turns into delusion; will drawn only from the group loses presence (§21). Pretend has to come back to reality (§18).

The fifth commitment is the closest the Atlas comes to a test of validity: knowledge counts when it has passed through others and come back. Even the punctuation carries this stance. The ellipses mark places where meaning is still open to revision (§4).

## Four axes

Any act of coordination can be placed on four axes the Atlas keeps returning to. Emergence and aperture separate the nine types best, so the map under the types uses those two.

| Axis | Positions | What the Atlas says |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Locus | Interior (will, faith, resonance, private intention) ↔ exterior (broadcast, ledger, aggregate) | Will is both deeply personal and communal; either side alone fails (§21) |
| Emergence | Woven (synthesis, harmony) ↔ ordered (turns, triage, governance) | Synthesis can't be scripted (§6); order is legitimate only when consensual and circular (§17) |
| Aperture | Contractive (resonance, invited groups, one-on-one repair) ↔ expansive (connectivity, field-wide broadcast) | Contraction is legitimate when it seeds and then shares back with the field (§11) |
| Tempo | Sequential · parallel · mobilized · paused | Turn-taking and fractal focus, with convergence in a crisis (§21); patience as signal (§13) |

## Nine types

Each type has its own way of knowing, its own product and its own typical failure.

| Type | How it knows | What it produces | How it typically fails |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Aggregative** (Maxim 4, §22) | Honest difference brought into contact; expanding rather than asserting | Truth, shared reality | Collapses into majority or authority |
| **Declarative** (§3, §12, §24, Maxims 3 and 7) | Participants announce intention, capacity, boundaries, refusal, start and stop | An accurate map of what is possible | Silence breeds vagueness; over-broadcasting monopolizes attention; sharing gets compelled |
| **Resonant** (§11) | Attunement and flow among a few | Trust, intimacy | Sequestering, cliques |
| **Synthetic** (§6) | Diversity colliding into spontaneous insight | Novelty, effectiveness | Homogenization; obligations imposed without support |
| **Narrative / traditional** (§20, §22, §26) | Story, norms, witnessing, the practice of paths | Continuity and orientation across time | The past acting as a tyrant; memory captured by one party |
| **Ordered, consensual** (§17, §23, §26) | Turn-taking, triage, decisions that narrow the options | Allocation, decisions, completion | Unjust hierarchy; fear written into rules |
| **Fractal** (§21) | Many domains focused in parallel, converging in a crisis (the organ metaphor) | Attention across the whole field at once | Tunnel vision; small groups left isolated |
| **Reparative** (§15, §16) | First-person accounts of harm, widening from the pair to a go-between to the field | Restored relationships, shared moral understanding | Abusers exploiting disclosure; false claims; a two-tier morality |
| **Ludic / imaginative** (§18) | Shared pretend: wonder, ask what if, then try | Possible futures | Pretend that never returns to reality; conflict disguised as play |

Consent and reciprocity are not types. They are the conditions every type has to meet (Maxims 1 and 2). Harmony is not a type either: §14 treats it as the outcome when signal, synthesis and resonance occur together.

The woven types are the ones the Atlas most admires; the ordered ones carry most of the routine work. Even the woven aggregative type needs an ordered rule before it can be read (tension 1).

## The shadow typology

Every genuine type has a counterfeit that imitates it. The Atlas calls these noise disguised as coordination (§25), and implies a four-part test for telling them apart:

1. **Choice.** Participants entered by choice, and withdrawal stays open (Maxim 1, §28).
2. **Return.** What is known passes through others and flows back to the field (§11, §18, §21, §22).
3. **Capacity.** It doesn't drain capacity or take time without consent (§12, §13).
4. **Humility.** It doesn't claim control over certainty (§7).

| Counterfeit | Imitates | Fails | Atlas basis |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Majority or crowd | Aggregative | 1, 2 | A sum is not an aggregate; consent can't be found in a crowd (Maxim 4, §28) |
| Assimilation, enforced unity | Synthetic | 1, 2 | Unity and homogenization don't generate synthesis (§6) |
| Cliques, permanent sequestering | Resonant | 2 | Withholding from the field breeds distrust and paranoia (§11) |
| Surveillance, compelled disclosure | Declarative | 1 | Observation is not care (§21); compelling someone to share is extortion (§28) |
| Fear-based hierarchy | Ordered | 1, 4 | Structures that run on fear or persecution are unjust (§17) |
| Sycophancy | Resonant | 2 | Affirming false positives validates delusion (§25) |
| The influencer | Fractal | 2, 3 | Being needed is not the same as being worthy (§21) |
| Controlled memory | Narrative / traditional | 2, 4 | Whoever controls memory entrenches a monopoly of power (§22) |
| Ungrounded pretend, war as play | Ludic | 2 | Pretend that never returns becomes illusion (§18) |
| Weaponized trust (the false participant) | Any type | 1, 2 | Trust used to amplify signal that benefits a few (§10, §17) |

Return is the condition counterfeits fail most often. That matches the fifth commitment: in the Atlas, knowledge that never passes through others is the root failure.

## Where the epistemology strains

Five tensions need resolving before the typology can be used. The first matters most, because every other type depends on the aggregative one.

1. **There is no rule for reading the aggregate.** Truth lives in the aggregate, not the majority (Maxim 4). Yet reality forms in the consensus of synthesis (§6), and consent can't be found in a crowd (§28). The Atlas never says how an aggregate is read without collapsing into majority, authority or crowd.
2. **The ledger conflicts with consent.** Maxim 4 makes consent to the record a condition of entry, and changing the record requires every participant's consent. That clashes with three other passages: consent can be withdrawn at any time (§28), some events should be left out (§4), and some memories may need releasing (§22). Unanimity also lets one participant block the correction of a false record.
3. **The way of spotting bad actors cuts against the text's own principles.** The warning signs in §17 and §21 closely follow most of the fifteen characteristics in [Jones and Okun's "White Supremacy Culture"](https://www.library.illinois.edu/arx/wp-content/uploads/sites/47/2017/05/White-Supremacy-Culture-Dismantling-Racism-A-Workbook-for-Social-Change-Groups-by-Kenneth-Jones-and-Tema-Okun-2001.pdf) (2001). That list was written to help organizations name and change their own norms; the Atlas uses it to flag individuals. The Atlas also holds that synthesis can't come from checklists (§6), that surveillance is paradoxical (§21), and that labels like psychosis can be used to manipulate (§7).
   - One sign matters most for an epistemology: an emphasis on objectivity or proof (§17). If asking for evidence counts as a red flag, the field switches off one of its own ways of correcting errors.
4. **Harm is the least clear domain but carries the heaviest powers.** §16 says the crucible of harm holds no clarity, yet judgments about harm decide termination (§26) and permit violence (§15). The text also splits on unintended harm: §15 calls it injustice, while §25 classifies it as an accident, not a sin.
5. **The role of order is unclear.** §6 says order cannot produce true synthesis, while §21 calls turn-taking an important piece of it. Reading order as scaffold rather than source reconciles the two, but the Atlas never says so.

## Implications for the covenant

The covenant's first job is epistemic: three procedures would resolve the first four tensions.

| Procedure | Question it answers | Resolves | Types it governs |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Aggregation rule | How does the field read truth from many partial signals without falling back on majority or authority? | Tension 1 | Aggregative, synthetic |
| Ledger amendment and redaction | Who can correct, annotate or withhold a record, and what happens when someone withdraws consent? | Tension 2 | Narrative / traditional, declarative |
| Harm determination | How is what happened established, by whom and at what scale, before termination is considered? | Tensions 3 and 4 | Reparative, ordered |

The fifth tension needs only a sentence in the covenant: order serves synthesis as a scaffold, and stays consensual and circular (§17).

§33 asks whether the field and its broadcasting come first, or the covenant. §30 says a covenant needs plural participants, so a small field has to exist first. Its first covenant work would be these procedures, before broadcasting is formalized, since declarations are held to the ledger (Maxim 4).

§25 asks for a visual model of activity in the field. Tagging each act of coordination with its type, its position on the four axes and its result on the four-part test would give that model its data.

## Open questions

- Should consent and reciprocity stay as validity conditions, or become types of their own?
- Are the warning signs meant for the field's self-reflection, or for screening individuals?
- Is the return test enough to separate unorthodox knowing from delusion, as §20 asks?
- Where should the covenant itself sit on the emergence axis: woven, ordered, or a declared mix?
